## pass-4-q4

- Fidelity: *voire que ne l'ayent voulu punir d'vn ſimple banniſſement* intensifies "not capital" — the ordinary Roman penalty was relegation, capital only where incest was joined (note d); the English inverted it so that incest earned mere banishment.: nay, that they did not wish to punish it with a simple banishment, unless when the adultery was joined with incest → nay, that they wished to punish it only with a simple banishment, unless when the adultery was joined with incest
- [unclear] left on *ſemblẽt peu, ou point meriter quelque excuſe*: the opinion Coras reports (and then rejects as impious) needs "seem to deserve some excuse"; the whole book offers no parallel to fix the dropped word. Possibly *peine* fell out before *quelque excuſe* ("little or no penalty, [but] some excuse").
- The heading of the next annotation (annot-084) is printed ANNOT. LXXXIII. for LXXXIV; the French keeps it, our numbering follows the true count.

## sweep-opus

- [check 5] Negation and restriction possibly inverted. French: 'Il semble à plusieurs qu'ils n'ayent point fait la peine de ce crime capitale: voire que ne l'ayent voulu punir d'vn simple bannissement: si ce n'est quand l'adultere estoit conioint auec inceste'. English: 'nay, that they wished to punish it only with a simple banishment, unless when the adultery was joined with incest'. Read literally, the French says they would not punish it even with simple banishment except when incest was involved ('voire' is building up the point). The English says banishment was the ordinary penalty and incest the exception. The English matches Roman law (relegation under the lex Iulia) and works if a 'que' (ne ... que) has dropped out, but nothing in the Notes records that reading. A reviewer should decide.
- [check 5] Ambiguous negation scope. French: '(ausquels tous, le Seigneur Dieu n'a point desparti la grace de se pouuoir contenir)' means God has not given all of them the grace of continence. English: '(to all of whom the Lord God has not dispensed the grace of being able to contain themselves)' reads naturally as 'none of them has it'. Suggest '(not all of whom has the Lord God granted the grace ...)' or '(to whom, not to all, the Lord God has dispensed ...)'.
- [check 7] Review pass 4 (4ad996a) reversed an explicit negation: old 'nay, that they did not wish to punish it with a simple banishment' became 'nay, that they wished to punish it only with a simple banishment'. The French *voire que ne l'ayent voulu punir d'vn ſimple banniſſement* has no *que* to make it *ne … que*. The reviewer's legal reasoning (relegation under the lex Iulia) may be right, but the literal reading also makes sense: not capital, not even banishment, except when incest was joined (incestuous adultery → deportation). Either restore the negation or keep the emendation and record in the Notes that the print reads *ne … d'vn*. Also see the q3 finding above.
