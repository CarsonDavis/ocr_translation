⟦p058⟧Although the law presumes of men that each one is good, of good life and of honest conversation [manner of living] {a}, and that none of them has the intention to do wrong {b}: nevertheless, in one who has once been wicked and caught in some wickedness, the law with great reason thinks and presumes him to be always such, in the same kind of wickedness {c}. As for example one who has at some time stolen, if he is again accused of theft, upon however small a proof from other conjecture there may be, one will easily presume him to be a thief today. And she who has once fornicated, that she still goes astray; and he who in the first accusation has been a calumniator, that he is so again in the second {d}. Of which and the like the reason is not hard to understand: for it is likely that in a will declared by deed or by word each one continues and perseveres {e}; inasmuch as a change of accident or quality (which consists in fact) is not easily presumed {f}. Rather, one draws argument and conjecture from the past to the present time and to the future {g}: as one whom one has once known to be rich, one presumes him still today rich; or poor, still poor; one who has been lord of a place, to be so afterwards; a subject, to be so still; and so of the like {h}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. XXXVII. on p058, correctly. The marker alphabet restarts at *a* and runs a–h; every note is keyed and every marker served. Wrong sorts and slips translated by sense: *ſeigueur* for *ſeigneur*; *ou* for *on* ("ou le preſumera"); *cil qu'il* for *cil qui*; *l'eſtré* with an acute (twice) for *l'eſtre*; in the margin *perſoſona* for *perſona* (note *h*), *poſtidens* for *poſſidens* (note *g*), and an italic capital *P* standing for the paragraph sign § in notes *a*, *d* and *g*. Reflow gaps read through: *ma lus* (*malus*), *ſu peruacuũ* (*ſuperuacuum*), *poſti dens*, *tranſa ctio*. *Bien viuant* → "of good life" as at annot-025; *honneſte conuerſation* → "honest conversation", glossed in brackets, since *conversation* here means one's manner of living, not talk; *meſchanceté* and *mauuaiſtié* are both rendered "wickedness" per the glossary; *ſurprins en* → "caught in"; *paillardé* → "fornicated" and *mal-verſe* → "goes astray" as at annot-002; *calomniateur* → "calumniator" as at annot-019; *changement d'accident, ou qualité (qui conſiſte en fait)* is the scholastic distinction between a change of substance and a change of accidental quality, which is a matter of fact and so must be proved, not presumed; *l'eſtre encores* → "to be so still".
