⟦p084⟧The saying of the Philosopher, renewed in our laws, is that for our affirming or denying, the truth changes not at all {a2}; which, as Cicero says in a certain place, has always had such power that by any art, device or contrivance of man whatsoever it has never been able to be overturned; and though it have no protector to take up its defence, it defends itself well enough of itself {b2}. And in another place: O how great, says he, is the force of truth, which against the wit, ruses and wiles of man defends itself easily, without the help or succour of any other {c2}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. LXIII. on p084. The body carries the markers {a2}, {b2} and {c2}, all on p084 and all served by keyed notes; the margin continues the lettering of the previous annotation with a second alphabet (the *a2*, *b2*, *c2* of the transcription). Reflow gap read through: *con tre* (*contre*). The `⟨alt⟩` at *rëuerſee,* / *rẽuerſee,* is resolved for reading B: the word is *renuersee* (*renversée*, "overturned"), which wants the tilde for the omitted *n*; a diaeresis on the *e* gives no word. The print's full stop after {a2} ("ne ſe change point {a2}. laquelle") is rendered by a semicolon, since the sentence continues. *La ſentence du Philoſophe* → "The saying of the Philosopher" — Aristotle, whom the schools call *the* Philosopher; the saying is that a thing is not so because we affirm it, but we affirm it truly because it is so (cf. *Categories* 12, 14b14–22; *Metaphysics* IX.10, 1051b6–9); *refriquee* → "renewed" — Coras's Latinism from *refricare*, "to rub up afresh, revive", the saying being taken up again in the law cited; *pour noſtre affermer, ou nier* → "for our affirming or denying" (Ulpian: *adsumptio … veritati non praeiudicat*); *par art, engin, ou machine quelconques d'homme* → "by any art, device or contrivance of man whatsoever"; *protecteur, qui prenne ſa defenſe* → "protector to take up its defence"; *ſe defend aſſez de ſoy-meſme* → "defends itself well enough of itself"; *l'eſperit, ruſes, & cauteles* → "the wit, ruses and wiles" (*ruse* per the glossary; *cautele*, a crafty trick); *ſans aide ni ſecours d'autruy* → "without the help or succour of any other" (Cicero's *per se ipsa*).

- {a2} (p084): **Digest 50.1 (*Ad municipalem et de incolis*), l. *Adsumptio* (D. 50.1.6 pr., Ulpian)** — l. aſſumptio. D. ad municipa. [*Adsumptio originis, quae non est, veritati non praeiudicat* — "the assumption of an origin one does not have does not prejudice the truth: for no one by lying makes himself of no origin, nor by claiming another's origin acquires it."]
- {b2} (p084): **Cicero, *In Vatinium* as cited — the sentence on the power of truth is not in the speech as it survives (the word *veritas* does not occur in it); unidentified, cf. *Pro Caelio* 63, cited next** — Ciceron en l'oraiſon con tre vatin. [Coras's paraphrase — truth never overturned by human art, and defending itself without a protector — is close to the *Pro Caelio* passage cited next; whether he has a distinct *In Vatinium* sentence in mind could not be confirmed.]
- {c2} (p084): **Cicero, *Pro Caelio* 63** — Ciceron en l'oraiſon pro M. Cœlio. [*O magnam vim veritatis, quae contra hominum ingenia, calliditatem, sollertiam contraque fictas omnium insidias facile se per se ipsa defendat!* — "O great power of truth, which against the wit, cunning and cleverness of men, and against the feigned snares of all, easily defends itself by itself."]
