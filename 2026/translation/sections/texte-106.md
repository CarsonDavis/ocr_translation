⟦p148⟧Which he retained tenaciously; and more ⟦p149⟧still, when the said Bertrande de Rols had received him for Martin Guerre her husband, from whom afterwards, conversing day and night together, it was easier for him to learn more, and to confirm himself better in what the others had told him — denying always, nevertheless, that he was a Necromancer, or had used any charms, enchantments, or any kind of magic.

## Notes
The print heads this section TEXTE. on p148. The body carries no marker and the margin no note. The page break falls inside *en|cor* ("more | still"); the English keeps the marker before "still". *Ladi.* = *ladite*, "the said". *Ce qu'il retenoit tenacemẽt* → "Which he retained tenaciously", the *ce que* being the information gathered in texte-102; *l'eut receu pour Martin Guerre ſon mari* → "had received him for Martin Guerre her husband" (cf. *recognoiſtre pour*, texte-70); *en conuerſant iour & nuit enſemble* → "conversing day and night together" (*conuerſer*, of living together, as at annot-102); *luy fut plus aiſé en apprendre d'auantage* → "it was easier for him to learn more"; *ſe confermer mieux* → "confirm himself better"; *Necromantien* → "Necromancer" per the glossary (*necromancien* → necromancer), Coras's capital kept; *charmes, enchantemens* → "charms, enchantments"; *aucune eſpece de magie* → "any kind of magic" (*magie* → magic). This is the denial that annot-105 refuses to credit; cf. the case file §2 for the confession of 16 September 1560, where "he denies necromancy".
