⟦p123⟧Those who give themselves up as prey to the immodest love of women let themselves be so consumed and overcome, little by little, by this mad passion that they sometimes lose their senses by it; and being unable to make a breach in the honour of the woman they pursue, in order to satisfy the lasciviousness of their unbridled desires and disordered appetites, they use a thousand blandishments, wiles and deceptions, nay often have recourse to force — whence is contracted the crime which we call Rape [*rapt*: in the law of the time, abduction as well as rape], and of which our gallant du Tilh remains sufficiently attainted and convicted. This Rape had been committed upon a married woman, and was therefore undoubtedly worthy of death {a}. For a woman is ravished not only ⟦p124⟧when she is violated and carried off from one place to another by force {b}, but also when she is seduced and suborned by ruses, cunning, baits and false persuasions {c}; and then the Rape is punished no less than if it had been committed and executed by force {d}: both because a will extorted by wiles, or by some fraud, does not warrant the trickster, nor cover his crime {e}; and also because to persuade craftily, with ruses and false and honeyed enticements, is no less than to force and constrain {f}. And so, in like terms, Ulpian the Jurisconsult answers that he who keeps a free person in his house, although it be with that person's consent, and he is content with it, if nevertheless such affection and contentment proceed from the cunning, subornation and false representations of him who keeps him, is no less culpable than if he kept him by force {g}. And thus are to be understood the texts of our laws which seem to require, for the crime of Rape, force and violence {h}. From this there follows a kind of excuse for the said de Rols: for a woman who has been forced cannot be charged with adultery, nor with any lechery {i}, even though for shame she has not at once declared it, and has expressly forbidden that it be told to her husband {k}; nay, the Emperor in these cases wills that the reputation of a woman of worth be inviolably and perpetually preserved to her {l}.

One `⟨alt⟩` in the body: *auoit⟨alt:auoir⟩ eſté commis* — read *auoit*, a finite clause after the full stop (*iceluy Rapt auoit eſté commis … & par ainſi indubitablement digne de mort*, with *eſtoit* understood); the infinitive would make a Latinate accusative-and-infinitive hanging from *conuaincu*, which the stop before *iceluy* tells against; the English is the same either way. Reflow gap read through: *conuain cu* (*conuaincu*). Wrong sorts translated by sense: *perſonue* for *perſonne*; *nox loix* for *nos loix*; in the margin *fi,* for *fin.* (note *a*). *Rapt* → "Rape" per the glossary, glossed in brackets on first use, since Coras's own definition here covers abduction and seduction as well as rape; Coras's capital kept. *ſe donnent en proye* → "give themselves up as prey"; *impudique* → "immodest"; *faire breſche à l'honneur* → "make a breach in the honour"; *blandices, cautelles, & deceptions* → "blandishments, wiles and deceptions" per the glossary; *ſe contracte le crime* → "is contracted the crime" (the legal *contrahere crimen*); *galant* → "gallant" (ironic); *attaint & conuaincu* → "attainted and convicted" per the glossary; *rauie* → "ravished"; *trãſduicte* → "carried off"; *ſeduite & ſubornee* → "seduced and suborned"; *fineſſes, apaſts* → "cunning, baits"; *ne garentit pas le trompeur* → "does not warrant the trickster" (*trompeur* → trickster per the glossary); *forfait* → "crime"; *allechemens faux, & emmiellez* → "false and honeyed enticements"; *faux donner entendre* → "false representations"; *coulpable* → "culpable"; *deſirer* (of a law) → "require"; *repriſe d'adultere* → "charged with adultery"; *paillardiſe* → "lechery"; *nommeement* → "expressly"; *femme de bien* → "woman of worth" per the glossary. The excuse Coras draws for Bertrande — that a woman deceived is as a woman forced — is the thread the following annotations (85, 86) pick up again; it is translated as he puts it.
