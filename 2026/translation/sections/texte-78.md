⟦p105⟧Seeing that this traitor had robbed her of her honour, and of the reputation of her chastity, she forthwith brought the prisoner to Justice, and pursued him so vigorously that by judgment of the Judge of Rieux he was condemned to lose his head and to be put in four quarters; and, not content, after the appeal lodged by him to the Parlement of Toulouse, she presented a petition to the said court, that she might be permitted to come there (for by the appeal she still remained under ar⟦p106⟧rest [*arreſtée*: confined by the court's order]) to represent the outrage that had been done to her, and to pursue it,

## Notes
The print heads this section TEXTE. on p105, after Annotation LXXVI (printed LXXVII); the section carries no marginal citations. It continues the record's account of Bertrande's conduct (*Voyant que…*) and ends on a comma in the print, the sentence running on into texte-76 (*Sur quoy…*); the comma is kept. The page break falls inside *ar|reſtée* and is kept at the corresponding word ("under ar⟦p106⟧rest"). Reflow gap read through: *cha ſteté*. Wrong sorts translated by sense: *mis & quatre quartiers* for *mis en quatre quartiers*; *ſentence* is the lower judge's *sentence* → "judgment" per §5. *Ce proditeur* → "this traitor" (as at texte-71); *deſrobé ſon honneur, & l'opinion de ſa chaſteté* → "robbed her of her honour, and of the reputation of her chastity" (*opinion* in the old sense, the esteem in which her chastity was held); *incontinent* → "forthwith"; *mit en Iuſtice le priſonnier* → "brought the prisoner to Justice" (Coras's capital kept; cf. *mettre en instance* → to sue); *l'a ſi viuement pourſuyui* → "pursued him so vigorously"; *condemné perdre la teſte, & eſtre mis en quatre quartiers* → "condemned to lose his head and to be put in four quarters" (the Rieux sentence, case file §2; Annotation LXXIX takes up the choice of death); *l'appel par luy interietté* → "the appeal lodged by him" (*interjeter appel*, the technical phrase); *preſenta requeſte* → "presented a petition" per the glossary (*requeste* → petition); *à ce qu'il luy fuſt permis ſ'en venir* → "that she might be permitted to come there"; *demeuroit par l'appel encor arreſtée* → "still remained under arrest", glossed in brackets: *arrest* in the second of the two senses Coras gives at annot-068, a magistrate's order confining a person to a place — Bertrande, as civil party in a case under appeal, was not free to leave; *remõſtrer* → "to represent" per the glossary (*remonstrer*); *l'outrage que luy a eſté fait* → "the outrage that had been done to her" (the French's present perfect *a eſté* is regularised to the narrative tense).
