⟦p106⟧This made great evidence of the good faith of the said de Rols, and that she never had the will to stray from her true consort and husband, nor in any way to violate the faith she owed him, although she had in fact carnally cohabited with the same du Tilh. For if a woman converses with another, thinking him to be her husband, so long as she is ignorant of it she cannot be called an adulteress, but only from the hour when she knows it and says no word of it {a}. Which the said de Rols did not do; but, on the contrary, from the moment she began to discover and to perceive the prodigious fraud in which the said du Tilh — cunning, subtle, malicious, and the most dissembling lecher that ever was — had so finely lulled her, she pursued him virtuously, sparing neither her goods nor her pains.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. LXXVIII. on p106; the transcription numbers it 77 (the printed numbering runs one ahead, flagged uncertain). The body carries one marker, {a}, served by a keyed note. The print's full stops inside running sentences (*ne pourra eſtre dite adultere. mais dés l'heure…*; *commença deſcouurir. & ſ'aperceuoir*) are regularised. This is the "more ample" discussion promised at annot-074 (*comme nous diſcourrons plus amplement ci apres*) of the wife who lies with a man she takes for her husband. *Faiſoit grande euidence de* → "made great evidence of"; *bonne foy* → "good faith" per the glossary (annot-011, of the deceived wife); *n'euſt onc volonté ſe foruoyer de* → "never had the will to stray from" (*forvoyer*, to go out of the way); *vray conſort, & mari* → "true consort and husband" per the glossary (annot-018); *violer… la foy qu'elle luy deuoit* → "violate the faith she owed him"; *charnellement cohabité* → "carnally cohabited" (cf. *cohabitation*); *conuerſe auec vn autre* → "converses with another", carnally, as at annot-074; *tandis qu'elle l'ignore* → "so long as she is ignorant of it"; *ne pourra eſtre dite adultere* → "cannot be called an adulteress"; *dés l'heure ſeulement qu'elle le ſçaura, & n'en dira mot* → "only from the hour when she knows it and says no word of it"; *deſcouurir. & ſ'aperceuoir de la prodigieuſe fraude* → "to discover and to perceive the prodigious fraud" (*prodigieux* → prodigious per the glossary; the same *ſ'apperceuoir de la fraude* as texte-74); *caut, ſubtil, malicieux* → "cunning, subtle, malicious"; *le plus diſſimulé paillard, qui fut oncques* → "the most dissembling lecher that ever was" (*paillard* → lecher, rogue; the first sense here, the fraud being carnal); *ſi finement l'auoit endormie* → "had so finely lulled her" (*endormie* → lulled, per the glossary, texte-10); *le pourſuyuit vertueuſement* → "pursued him virtuously" (with virtue, i.e. with resolution); *ſans pardonner à ſes biens, ni à ſes peines* → "sparing neither her goods nor her pains".

- {a} (p106): **Gratian, *Decretum*, c. *Si virgo*, at "C. 33 q. 2" as printed — the chapter stands in the modern division at C. 34 qq. 1–2 c. 5; the same citation as annot-075 {r}** — c ſi virgo. xxxiij. q. ij. [The woman who, deceived, lies with a man she believes to be her husband commits no adultery while the error lasts; she becomes an adulteress only if, learning the truth, she continues and says nothing — the rule Coras states.]
