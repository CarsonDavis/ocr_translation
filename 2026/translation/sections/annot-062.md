⟦p082⟧Inasmuch as the wicked, the too licentious and the virulent have never spared their stinking and infected tongues to detract from people of worth and virtue, it has often been seen to happen that by their venomous slanders a man of worth has been defamed of a deed which he would not have thought of for his life; and nevertheless such a rumour, unjustly spread abroad, marked him in the eyes of many persons too prone to think ill of their neighbour — upon whose opinion alone, however, it would not be reasona⟦p083⟧ble to judge or condemn that man. And so in civil matters: for a wicked man could easily cause a rumour of false things to be sown, to serve his cause. Wherefore our laws have wisely ordained that proof by rumour and common repute is not full proof {a}, save in certain cases collected by our interpreters in the common places {b}: as when it is a question of proving the bounds and limits of lands {c}; or of showing certain old and ancient facts exceeding the memory of men {d}; or else of making it appear that Jean is the son of Antoine, Pierre the son of François, or any other filiation {e}; or of proving the death of someone {f}; or of putting in evidence some other thing which cannot be proved save with great difficulty. And although in civil matters it is received that rumour and renown make a half-proof, nevertheless in criminal causes (of which we are speaking), or indeed in civil ones that are high and grave, this has no place {h}. Wherefore the judge could not then join this half-proof with one witness, to make it full and entire {i}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. LXI. on p082. The marker alphabet restarts at *a*; the body carries {a}–{f}, {h} and {i}, all on p083, and the margin of p083 carries nine notes keyed *a*–*i*. Note *g* has no marker in the body (the transcription marks it an orphan); by its content — Accursius and Salicetus on common repute as proof — it serves either the clause "or of putting in evidence some other thing which cannot be proved save with great difficulty" or the following clause on the half-proof in civil matters, both of which stand unmarked between {f} and {h}; it is listed below as an orphan. The page break falls inside *raiſonna|ble* and is kept at the corresponding word ("reasona⟦p083⟧ble"). Reflow gap read through: *quel ques* (*quelques*). The print's full stop after {b} ("és lieux communs {b}. comme") and after {a}-style periods within the list ("{c}. Ou", "{d}. Ou", "{e}. Ou", "{f}. Ou") are rendered by a colon and semicolons, since the enumeration continues; its *d'Antoine,:* has a stray comma before the colon. *Partant que* → "Inasmuch as"; *virulẽts* → "virulent"; *puante & infecte langue* → "stinking and infected tongues"; *detracter des gens de bien & de vertu* → "to detract from people of worth and virtue"; *venimeuſes maledicences* → "venomous slanders"; *diffamé d'vn fait* → "defamed of a deed"; *le notoit* → "marked him" (Latin *notare*, to brand with a mark of infamy); *trop procliues à mal ſentir de leur prochain* → "too prone to think ill of their neighbour"; *ſainctement ordonné* → "wisely ordained" (*sainctement*, 'in holy wise', said of a law well made); *la preuue par bruit & fame n'eſtre pas entiere* → "proof by rumour and common repute is not full proof" (*preuve entière*, full proof, as against *demie preuve*, half-proof); *nos interpretes* → "our interpreters" and *lieux communs* → "common places" per the glossary (annot-005, annot-044); *bornes & limites des terres* → "bounds and limits of lands"; *excedans la memoire des hommes* → "exceeding the memory of men"; *filation* → "filiation" per the glossary (annot-041, where the same three exceptions appear); *bruit & renommee* → "rumour and renown" (the glossary has "rumour and common repute" for *bruit & fame*, which is kept for that pair; *renommee* is here distinguished); *demie preuue* → "half-proof"; *ciuiles, hautes, & graues* → "civil ones that are high and grave" (the *causae arduae* of the doctors); *conioindre … auec vn teſmoin* → "join … with one witness" (the doctrine that *fama* plus a single witness may make up full proof, which Coras denies in criminal causes).
