⟦p109⟧That from which appeal had been taken was the judgment of the Judge of Rieux, by which the same du Tilh was condemned to lose his head and afterwards to be cut into four quarters; which was quashed by the Court, because this kind of death seemed to it, for so prodigious and abominable a traitor, heaped up with every kind of vice, [unclear: the print gives no predicate here — *luy ſembloit pour vn ſi prodigieux … proditeur …*; "too honourable" is evidently meant] singu⟦p110⟧larly because, although the difference laid down by our laws, as to the imposition of penalties, between nobles and those of low condition {a} is not strictly kept in France, where that general rule is held more tenaciously, that crimes and misdeeds cast off and annul all dignity and all privileges {b}: nevertheless in judgments of death the French have religiously observed at all times that nobles are beheaded, and the others hanged {c}. Some further testify that it is observed in France that if a person, however noble and illustrious he may be, commits some notable treachery and treason, he is not beheaded, but is hanged on a gibbet, and on gibbet-forks higher and more raised than the others {d}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. LXXX. on p109 (the print's numbering runs one ahead of the section id). The marker alphabet is *a*–*d*, all keyed in the margin of p110 and all served. The page break falls inside *ſingulie|rement* and is kept at the corresponding word ("singu⟦p110⟧larly"). The main sentence is anacoluthic in the print: *ceſte eſpece de mort luy ſembloit pour vn ſi prodigieux … proditeur …, ſingulierement que …* has no predicate after *ſembloit*; the sense required by the rest of the annotation (nobles beheaded, commoners hanged) is that beheading seemed to the Court too honourable a death for du Tilh, and the gap is marked `[unclear]` rather than filled. Reflow gaps read through: *ob ſerué*, *pro dition*. Wrong sorts and slips translated by sense: *l'ont tient* for *l'on tient*; *le François* for *les François*; in the margin *quaſ* for *quæſt.* (note *a*), *Indi gnat.* broken across lines (note *b*), *qnidã* for *quidã* (note *d*), and an italic capital *P* for the paragraph sign § in note *c*. *Ce dont auoit eſté appelé* → "That from which appeal had been taken", picking up texte-78; *ſentence* → "judgment" per §5; *perdre la teſte, & apres eſtre mis en quatre quartiers* → "to lose his head and afterwards to be cut into four quarters" (the Rieux sentence, §2–3); *caſſée* → "quashed"; *prodigieux* → "prodigious" per the glossary; *proditeur* → "traitor" (cf. *proditoirement* → treacherously); *comblé en toute eſpece de vices* → "heaped up with every kind of vice"; *l'impoſition des peines* → "the imposition of penalties"; *ceux de baſſe condition* → "those of low condition"; *eſtroitement gardée* → "strictly kept"; *reiettent, & aneantiſſent toute dignité, & tous les priuileges* → "cast off and annul all dignity and all privileges"; *religieuſement obſerué* → "religiously observed"; *decapitez* → "beheaded"; *prodition & trahiſon notable* → "notable treachery and treason"; *on ne la decole point* → "he is not beheaded"; *fourches* → "gibbet-forks" (the *fourches patibulaires*, the permanent gallows of a lord's justice, whose number of pillars marked his rank).
