⟦p052⟧In the second place, there are witnesses who at other times have contracted with the said du Tilh, or been present at his contracts as instrumentary witnesses [*tesmoins numeraires*: the witnesses required by law to make up the number for an instrument]; and the instruments are produced. For the third, all these witnesses almost agree that Martin Guerre was taller and darker, a man slender of body and of legs, a little stooped, carrying his head between his two shoulders, the chin forked and a little raised upward, in whom the lower lip drooped a little downward, having small teeth, the nose broad and flat, an ulcer on the face, and a scar over the right eyebrow; whereas nevertheless the prisoner is short, stocky and thickset of body, having a thick leg, is neither flat-nosed nor stooped, and much less has all the said scars.

## Notes
The print heads this section TEXTE. on p052. This is the body-shape evidence on which Davis's argument (case file §10) rests; the particulars are kept exactly as Coras gives them. *Camus* is "flat-nosed" (Ringold and Lewis: "a large and flattened nose"); *voulté* (= *voûté*) "stooped"; *trappe* (= *trapu*) "stocky"; *fourni de corps* "thickset of body"; *leſd.* = *lesdites*.
