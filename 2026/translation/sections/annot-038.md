⟦p059⟧These witnesses, although they did not equal the number of the others, nevertheless seemed to be more credible for several considerations. The first, because they affirmed that the prisoner was Martin Guerre, and the others denied it. Now it is certain that to two witnesses alone who affirm something, more faith is given than to a thousand who deny {a}. The second, because the principal of these witnesses are his own and nearest kinsfolk: and even four sisters, who obstinately affirmed the prisoner to be their brother. And everyone knows well that kinsfolk, and singularly sisters, have without comparison a better and more perfect knowledge of those who belong to them in so near a degree of kinship than all other persons {b}. The third, because the witnesses who depose for the prisoner testify to things nearer to verisimilitude. Inasmuch as he had been received as Martin Guerre by all those of the town, and even by his said sisters and nearest kinsfolk; nay, by the wife of the said Martin, with whom he had cohabited three years, and had two children: in which interval, so long, it is not probable that the said de Rols would not have recognised him for a stranger, if the prisoner had not truly been Martin Guerre. The fourth and last, because these witnesses depose for the defendant, and in favour both of the marriage and of the children who have issued from it; in which cases, if several Judges were in conflict of opinions, the opinion and judgment of those who favoured either the accused or the {c} marriage would always prevail; and so it seems that Hermogenian the Jurisconsult wrote and taught it, when there is contradiction of witnesses. {d}

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOT. XXXVIII. on p059, correctly. The marker alphabet restarts at *a* and runs a–d; every note is keyed and every marker served. The print sets the marker {c} before its word with a point after it ("ou le {c}. ma riage"), and the marker {d} after the final full stop; both are kept where the print has them. Notes *c* and *d* are printed several lines above their markers, which does not affect the English, keyed by marker. Wrong sorts translated by sense: *affer moyen* (the print has no tilde or *t*; the parallel *affermoyẽt* four lines below gives the reading "they affirmed"); *premeuu*, evidently *preuenu* (a turned *n* and a wrong sort), translated "the accused" — the pairing *ou le preuenu, ou le mariage* answers to the *favor rei* and *favor matrimonii* of note *c*; in the margin *Aecurſe* for *Accurſe* (note *a*). Reflow gaps read through: *com paraiſon*, *vray ſem blable*, *teſ moins*, *ma riage*; *meillieure* for *meilleure*, *deulx* for *deux*, *troys* for *trois*. The French *ſembloit* (singular) with a plural subject is rendered "seemed". The conditionals *auroit eſté receu*, *auroit cohabité* are the reportive conditional of a record ("as the witnesses said, he had been received"), rendered by the plain past. *Les propres & plus prochains parens* → "his own and nearest kinsfolk" (*parens* → kinsfolk per the glossary); *ſingulierement* → "singularly"; *veriſimilitude* → "verisimilitude" per the glossary; *deffendeur* → "the defendant"; *l'aduis & la ſentence* → "the opinion and judgment" (the *sententia* of a judge in the Roman sense, his vote); *contradiction de teſmoins* → "contradiction of witnesses"; *Hermogenien Iuriſconſulte* → "Hermogenian the Jurisconsult" (Hermogenianus, the late-classical jurist of the *Iuris epitomae*).

P. ſi plu res. D. de re cep. arb. [The gloss's commonplace that two witnesses who affirm are believed before a thousand who deny, a negative not being susceptible of direct proof.
