⟦p076⟧A child in Latin is called INFANS until the age of seven years {a}, as it were unable to speak (*quasi nescius fari*), that is to say, as not knowing how to speak: because, being in this tender age, he can scarcely yet untie his tongue to pronounce the words distinctly. Whence Chrysippus said that children who are already beginning to babble and to pronounce some words may be said almost to speak, but in truth they say nothing, nor speak at all. Thus one must not be astonished if, in the matter of which we treat, the prisoner, who had been brought very small, and at the age of little more than two years, from the Basque country, had not retained the language, which he never understood nor ever knew how to speak.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. LVI. on p076. The marker alphabet restarts at *a*: one note, *a*, keyed and served. Reflow gap read through: *aa ge*. *Iuſqu'a* without grave is the print's. The Latin tag *quaſi neſcius fari* is translated in the text with the Latin in parentheses, being three words; Coras then glosses it himself ("c'eſt à dire comme ne ſçachant parler"), and the gloss is kept. The capitals of INFANS are the print's and are kept. *Ce bas aage* → "this tender age"; *deſnouër ſa langue* → "untie his tongue"; *diſertement* → "distinctly"; *gazouiller* → "babble" (of birds, to twitter — the same figure as *ramage* at annot-035); *peuuent eſtre dits preſque parler* → "may be said almost to speak"; *le priſonnier* → "the prisoner", kept as Coras writes it, though the man meant is Martin Guerre, whose identity the prisoner claims; *du pays des Baſcouz* → "from the Basque country", per §4 of the case file; *qui n'entendit, ni ne ſceut iamais parler* → "which he never understood nor ever knew how to speak". The saying of Chrysippus is uncited in the margin; it is preserved by Varro, *De lingua Latina* 6.56 (Chrysippus denies that infants beginning to utter words speak — they only "as it were speak", as the image of a man is not a man).
