⟦p099⟧Let the judges here take heed how dangerous it is, and full of peril, singularly in criminal matters, where the honour and the life of a man are treated of, to found a judgment upon the deposition of witnesses: who oftentimes pertinaciously assure false things to be true {a}, from which afterwards they are constrained to depart. Let the judges see also how much surer it is, even for a sovereign judgment, not to rest simply upon the saying of the witnesses, nor upon their deposition as it is found written, but to make them come in person, to hear them, to see and contemplate their gestures and countenances, to interrogate them, to make them render a reason for the whole exactly: for I believe that, in so doing, the way would be cut off to many malignities, calumnies and conspiracies of witnesses, who would not be so easy and prone to depose falsely, for the reverence, honour and majesty of a sovereign court before which they would have to answer. And this is what the Emperor Hadrian wrote to Junius Rufinus, proconsul of Macedonia: that he wished to believe witnesses, and not their testimony or depositions; for the faith and authority of witnesses who are present is other, and greater beyond comparison, than that of depositions which are only read and recited {b}, and most often written and dictated more at the appetite of some bad fellow of a commissioner, or of some scribbler of a clerk, than according to the intention and will of the witness. And Callistratus the Jurisconsult, pursuing Hadrian's argument on this matter, says that, upon the accusation which Alexander made against one called Aper of I know not what crimes, forasmuch as Alexander did not produce witnesses, but wished to use only their depositions, Hadrian answered that the testimonies had no place with him, and that he would give them no credit: "for I wish myself," said he, "to interrogate the witnesses" {c}. And this is, in my opinion, what our Justinian has left written, ⟦p100⟧that in criminal matters, where the peril is greater (for it concerns the honour and the life of a man), the witnesses must be presented and offered personally to the judge {d}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. LXXIIII. on p099; the transcription numbers it 73 (the printed numbering runs one ahead, flagged uncertain). It is the annotation on proof by witnesses promised at annot-026 {b} ("En l'annotation lXXiij") — Coras's own cross-reference gives the number of the count, not of the print, which supports the transcription's numbering. The body carries the markers {a}–{d}, {a}–{c} on p099 and {d} on p100, each served by a keyed note. The page break falls between *eſcrit,* and *qu'és* and is kept there. Reflow gaps read through: *eſ crites*. Wrong sorts and slips translated by sense: *teſmoios* for *teſmoins*, *qui ſon* for *qui ſont*, *depſitions* for *depoſitions*. Five `⟨alt⟩` markers fall in this section (four in the body, two in the margin); the choices are in the batch report. *Aduiſent ici les iuges* → "Let the judges here take heed" (jussive, as *Contemplent* at annot-026); *plein de peril* → "full of peril"; *où ſe traitte de l'honneur & de la vie de l'homme* → "where the honour and the life of a man are treated of"; *aſſeoir iugement* → "to found a judgment" per the glossary (annot-026); *pertinacement* → "pertinaciously" (cf. *pertinacité*); *ſe departir* → "to depart (from)", of a witness retracting; *iugement ſouuerain* → "sovereign judgment"; *geſtes & contenances* → "gestures and countenances" (*contenance* → countenance); *leur faire rendre raiſon du tout* → "to make them render a reason for the whole" (the witness's *causa scientiae*, cf. *rendre raison de son dire*); *ſeroit retrenché le chemin à* → "the way would be cut off to"; *malignitez, calomnies & conſpirations* → "malignities, calumnies and conspiracies"; *faciles & procliues* → "easy and prone"; *court ſouueraine* → "sovereign court"; *Iune Ruffin* → Junius Rufinus (Coras's form in the headnote; the Digest's *Iunius Rufinus*); *ni depoſitions* → "or depositions"; *leuës, & recitées* → "read and recited"; *à l'appetit de* → "at the appetite of"; *vn mauuais garçon de commiſſaire* → "some bad fellow of a commissioner" (*commissaire* → commissioner); *vn brouillaçon de greffier* → "some scribbler of a clerk" (*greffier* → clerk of court per §5; *brouillaçon*, from *brouiller*, a scrawler); *Iuriſconſulte* → "Jurisconsult" per the glossary; *pourſuyuant l'argumẽt* → "pursuing the argument"; *n'auoyẽt point lieu enuers luy* → "had no place with him" (the Digest's *quibus apud me locus non est*); *n'y donneroit point de foy* → "would give them no credit"; *noſtre Iuſtinien* → "our Justinian"; *repreſentez, & offerts perſonnellement* → "presented and offered personally".
