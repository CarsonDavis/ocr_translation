⟦p054⟧If these witnesses had not been singular, each deposing to his own fact, and well objected to, this proof alone would indeed have been sufficient to put him to the torture [*géhenne*]. For although a confession of a crime, made out of judgment, is not sufficient to condemn a man, without a new trial, or else extraordinarily at the discretion of the Judge, as a fool, vainglorious and overweening, having boasted of doing ill {a}: nevertheless it makes a sufficient indication for putting such a rustic, who has confessed, to the torture {b}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT, XXXII. on p054, with a comma after ANNOTAT where the other heads carry a point; the number is right and the heading is translated normally. The alphabet restarts at this annotation: two notes, *a* and *b*, both with markers. *Singuliers* is the jurists' *testes singulares*, witnesses who each depose to a different fact and so do not concur; *bien reprochez* is "well objected to" per the glossary (*reproches* = objections to witnesses). *Géine* (= *géhenne*, the torture) is glossed on first use. The clause "ſans nouueau proces, ou ſoit extraordinairement à l'arbitre du Iuge" is rendered literally: an extrajudicial confession does not convict unless a new trial is had, or unless the confessor is punished extraordinarily, at the judge's discretion, as a boastful fool. *Mettre à la torture* → "to put to the torture"; cf. the glossary's *question* → judicial torture.

- {a} (p054): **Decretals, X 5.31.9 (*De excessibus praelatorum et subditorum*, c. *Quam sit grave*)** — c. quàm ſit graue. de ex. ceſ. prelato [Cited for the rule that a confession made out of court does not of itself convict, but may draw an extraordinary penalty on the boaster; the print's *prelato* has a plain e for *praelato*.]
- {b} (p054): **Accursius (*Accurſe*), the Gloss on Digest 48.5.26 (old numbering 48.5.25) (*Ad legem Iuliam de adulteriis coercendis*, l. *Capite quinto*; Ulpian)** — Accurſe en la l. capite quinto- D. de adult, [The extrajudicial confession as an *indicium* sufficient for torture.]
