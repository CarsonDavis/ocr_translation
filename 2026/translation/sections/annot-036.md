⟦p057⟧The witnesses reported that this du Tilh was accustomed to swear by the head, body, blood and wounds of our Lord: which is what is commonly called Blaspheming; which is nothing else, according to the exposition of the Theologians and Canonists, than to attribute to God body, members and other things that do not befit him, or else to detract from what belongs to him {a}. Although in truth Blasphemy ought to be defined otherwise: for, leaving aside the interpretation of the Hebrews and Cabalists, who say that to blaspheme the name of God is nothing else than to express that great and ineffable name, the Tetragrammaton (which ought not to be pronounced or written), by its letters and characters — it must be understood that Blasphemy is a name drawn from the Greek, which signifies detestation, insult, contempt, malediction and vituperation. Whence to Blaspheme, properly speaking, is to despise, detest and treat with contumely God, or his son Jesus Christ {b}, provoking them with reproaches and insults {c}. Thus when the Jews threw stones at our Saviour Jesus Christ, they said they were stoning him not for good works (as the Lord justly complained) but for Blasphemy {d}. And such Blasphemers, by the ancient law, were stoned by the people {e}; and from that law our civil constitutions, drawn from it, condemn Blasphemers to death: which ought to be religiously observed, and (as Saint Gregory says) without using any mercy {f}; provided, however, that they are accustomed and hardened in doing so {g}. Otherwise, for one, two and three times, the law does not put them to death (not wishing to draw too easily to the penalty of death a slip and looseness of language {h}), but punishes them extraordinarily, according to the quality of the persons and other circumstances observed, the whole being left to the discretion of the judge {i}: inasmuch as the law thinks that such unaccustomed blasphemies proceed more from some passion, lightness of mind or bad ⟦p058⟧upbringing than from a pernicious will {k}.

TEXT

So much so that if he dreamed up this new impudence and imposture, one need not be astonished at it.
