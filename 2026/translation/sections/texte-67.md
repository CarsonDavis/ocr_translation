⟦p085⟧And by the same means answer is made to this, that during the said three years the said de Rols made no complaint of it, but rather obstinately maintained, against those who said ⟦p086⟧the contrary, that this same du Tilh was Martin Guerre her husband; nay, when he was under prosecution [*en prevention*: a judge having taken cognizance of a matter against him] for the same deed before the Seneschal of Toulouse, she went often to see him, giving him succour of money and of other necessary things — remaining, as is to be presumed, always in this error.

## Notes
The print heads this section TEXTE. on p085, after Annotation LXV (the print's LXVI); the section carries no marginal citations. It continues the record's answers to the points in the prisoner's favour (texte-62 and texte-63 answer Bertrande's refusal to swear; this answers her three years' silence). The page break falls between *diſoyent* and *le contraire* and is kept at the corresponding words ("who said ⟦p086⟧the contrary"). The `⟨alt⟩` at *qu'icceluy⟨alt:qu'iceluy⟩* is taken as *qu'iceluy* ("that this same"); *icceluy* is a doubled sort, and *iceluy* is the word Coras uses throughout. The print's *leſd.* and *lad.* are its abbreviations of *leſdits* and *ladite*. *Par meſme moyen* → "by the same means"; *eſt reſpondu à ce que* → "answer is made to this, that"; *ne ſ'en eſt plainte* → "made no complaint of it"; *ains defendoit obſtineement* → "but rather obstinately maintained" (*defendre* here in the sense of upholding a position, as in *defendre comme ſon mari* at annot-066); *voire* → "nay"; *luy eſtant en preuention de meſme faict deuant le Seneſchal de Toloſe* → "when he was under prosecution for the same deed before the Seneschal of Toulouse", with the French term glossed in brackets on this first use: *prevention* is the cognizance a judge takes of a criminal matter, so that the accused is *en prevention* once a court has seized of his case; *de meſme faict* is rendered literally, "for the same deed" — texte-45 said the Seneschal's prosecution was *pour autres faits*, "for other deeds", and the reviewer may wish to weigh whether *meſme faict* here means the same imposture or is used loosely ("likewise"); the case file §2 records the secondary literature's charge (arson) as unverified; *ſecours d'argent* → "succour of money" (Ringold and Lewis: "help with money"); *demeurant … touſiours en ceſt erreur* → "remaining … always in this error" (*erreur* the legal *error personae*, per the glossary at texte-10); *comme eſt à preſumer* → "as is to be presumed", the presumption Annotation LXVI then expounds.
