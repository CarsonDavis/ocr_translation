MARY OF THE FACTS.

Martin Guerre, of the place of Artigat in Gascony, having a fair young wife, called Bertrande de Rols, goes off to the war, and remains eight years absent: which having passed, Arnaud du Tilh, calling himself Martin Guerre, presents himself to the sisters, uncle, and relatives of the said Martin, together with the said de Rols, his wife: who all, by reason of the great likeness that there was between him and the said absent Martin, and of the true tokens that he gave to each of them of all things, easily persuade themselves that he is Martin Guerre, and receive him as such: and he is recognised by all the inhabitants of the said Artigat, and even by the said de Rols, with whom he cohabits three years as husband, and by his doings has two children. After the imposture is somewhat discovered, he is made prisoner by authority of the judge of Rieux, and at last condemned to lose his head. From which he appeals to the Parlement of Toulouse, where he is brought and heard: maintaining always that he was Martin Guerre, as likewise did the four sisters, and their husbands, brothers-in-law of the said Martin, together with thirty or forty witnesses. But because several others, on the contrary, affirmed him to be Arnaud du Tilh, or else were in doubt of it, and dared affirm neither the one nor the other, on account of the great resemblance of the prisoner to the said Martin and to du Tilh: the court was in marvellous perplexity. And as they were about to judge the case, Martin Guerre arrives: who nevertheless, confronted with the said du Tilh, remains almost vanquished: so much better did the impostor know how to paint over his lies than the other to help himself with the truth. Whereupon the judges, still more uncertain, have the sisters and certain other witnesses brought: by whom the newcomer is remarked and recognised as Martin Guerre, and the imposture made evident. Whence there follows a decision, that the said du Tilh shall be hanged, and his body burnt: the children nonetheless begotten of his doings and of the said de Rols declared legitimate.
