⟦p073⟧And as to Carbon Barrau, uncle of the said du Tilh, and other witnesses who particularise so closely the facts against the said prisoner, they have been sharply and validly objected to, and the objections found good and well proved. The saying of the soldier counts for nothing either: for he has not been heard, but it is others who depose that they heard him say it.

## Notes
The print heads this section TEXTE. on p073, after Annotation LII; the section carries no marginal citations. The print spells the uncle *Corbon Barrau*; the case file's and the earlier sections' *Carbon Barrau* is kept (§3), the print's form being recorded here. Wrong sorts and slips translated by sense: *poi nt* for *point*; a point after *trouuez* where the sense runs on ("found good"). *Particulariſent de ſi pres les faicts* → "particularise so closely the facts" (give the facts in such particular detail); *viuement, & vallablement reprochez* → "sharply and validly objected to" (*reprocher* → to object against per §5, never "reproach"); *les obiects* → "the objections" (the grounds of objection, the *reproches* themselves); *trouuez bons, & bien prouuez* → "found good and well proved"; *le dire du ſoldat* → "the saying of the soldier" (the soldier from Rochefort, case file §3, who denounced the impostor at Artigat but was never himself heard); *n'y fait rien* → "counts for nothing"; *il n'a point eſté ouy* → "he has not been heard" (*ouïr* → to hear per §5); *depoſent le luy auoir ouy dire* → "depose that they heard him say it" (the hearsay of Annotation LIII).
