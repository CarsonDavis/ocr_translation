⟦p087⟧Great certainly, and marvellous, is the goodness, grace and mercy of our God: which, when it pleases Him to pour it out upon us, there is no ruse, cunning or malignity of men, no inventions, wiles or malice of Satan, that can hinder it or offer it any resistance — as this deed here, after countless others, makes plain: two poor innocents, such as were Bertrande de Rols and Pierre Guerre, who by the strong hand of the Almighty were delivered from the imminent peril of death in which they (as calumniators) were placed; and the impudently measureless imposture of this wretched cheat du Tilh, as by a miracle, made manifest and discovered. And in truth it was indeed a miracle to make Martin Guerre appear — at whose expense all these pitiful games had been played — at the point when this case was about to be judged, in which the judges found themselves in incredible perplexity, and perhaps in danger of giving a judgment which would not altogether have answered to the justice of the cause, because the matters (in the opinion of many) were disposed more to the advantage of the prisoner, and against the said Pierre Guerre and de Rols: but the all-good and mighty God, who with His keen and piercing eye sees all things,

(The eye of God is keen; it sees all things — *Oculus Dei acutus est, videt omnia*)

and, being the sole searcher of men, sounds their deeds, contemplates and weighs their works {a}, and who has left it written by the learned pen of His Prophets and Evangelists that there is nothing so covered, so secret, nor so hidden that in the end He does not reveal it and bring it to light {b} — would not permit that so strange and impudently brazen a trickery, so scandalous an effrontery, so horrible and monstrous an imposture should remain concealed and unknown.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOT. LXVIII. on p087; it is the sixty-seventh in the running count (the print's numbers run one ahead of the count from Annotation LX onwards), and the transcription flags the number as uncertain. The marker alphabet restarts at *a*; the body carries {a} and {b}, both on p087, each served by a keyed note. The Latin sentence *Oculus Dei acutus eſt, videt omnia* is set by the print in parentheses as a line of its own between two paragraph breaks, interrupting Coras's sentence; it is kept so, translated with the Latin beside it, and the sentence resumes after it as in the French ("and, being the sole searcher of men…"). The sentence that begins *mais le tout bon & puiſſant Dieu* finds its verb only at *ne voulut permettre*, after the two relative clauses; the English keeps the suspension with dashes. The print sets a point after {a} ("œuures, {a}. & qui") and after {b} ("euidence {b}. ne voulut"), rendered by commas and a dash, since the sentence continues. The `⟨alt⟩` at *œuures,⟨alt:œuures⟩* (comma or no comma before {a}) does not affect the translation and is left open; the `⟨alt⟩` in note *a*, *Ther⟨alt:Theſ⟩ ſaloniſsiens*, is taken as *Theſſaloniſsiens* (Thessalonians). Wrong sorts and slips translated by sense: *perplexiré* for *perplexité*; *diſpoſez* (masculine) agreeing with *les affaires*. Reflow gaps read through: *maligni té*, *puiſ ſant*, *per mettre*, *Pier re*. *Grande certes eſt, & eſmerueillable* → "Great certainly, and marvellous, is" (*esmerveillable* → marvellous per the glossary); *ruſe, aſtuce, ni malignité* → "ruse, cunning or malignity" (*ruse* → ruse, *astuce* → cunning per the glossary); *inuentions, cautelle, ou malice de Satã* → "inventions, wiles or malice of Satan" (*cautelles* → wiles); *cõme ce faict ici. Apres infinis autres met en euidence* → "as this deed here, after countless others, makes plain" (*mettre en évidence*, cf. *venir en evidence* → to come to light in the glossary; the print's full stop after *ici* is a slip, since *met* has *ce faict* for its subject); *deux pauures innocẽs* → "two poor innocents"; *par la main forte du tout puiſſant* → "by the strong hand of the Almighty"; *l'eminẽt peril de la mort, ou ils (cõme calomniateurs) eſtoient poſez* → "the imminent peril of death in which they (as calumniators) were placed" (*calomniateur* → calumniator per the glossary: the talion for calumny is death where the charge was capital, as texte-19 and annot-019 set out); *l'impudẽment deſmeſurée impoſture* → "the impudently measureless imposture"; *mal-heureux affrõteur* → "wretched cheat" (*affronteur* → cheat); *piteux ieux* → "pitiful games"; *incroyable perplexité* → "incredible perplexity"; *vn iugemẽt qui n'euſt pas du tout reſpõdu à la iuſtice de la cauſe* → "a judgment which would not altogether have answered to the justice of the cause" (the Court's own *iugement*, as *l'aduis & la ſentence des Iuges* at annot-038, not the lower judge's *sentence*); *les affaires … eſtoient plus diſpoſez à l'auantage du priſonnier* → "the matters … were disposed more to the advantage of the prisoner" — Coras's admission that the court leaned to acquittal, which the case file §2 and §10 note; *œil aigu & perſpicace* → "keen and piercing eye"; *ſeul ſcrutateur des hõmes* → "sole searcher of men"; *ſonde leurs faicts, contemple & balance leurs œuures* → "sounds their deeds, contemplates and weighs their works"; *la docte plume* → "the learned pen"; *piperie* → "trickery", *affrontemẽt* → "effrontery" per the glossary (texte-05, annot-007); *impudemment effrontée* → "impudently brazen"; *celée & incogneuë* → "concealed and unknown".

- Verse (p087): **A Latin sentence, source not identified: *Oculus Dei acutus est, videt omnia*. It is not a scriptural verse in this form; cf. Ecclesiasticus 23:19 (Vulgate 23:28), *oculi Domini multo plus lucidiores sunt super solem, circumspicientes omnes vias hominum*, and Proverbs 15:3** — Oculus Dei acutus eſt, videt omnia. [Possibly a schoolroom or emblem tag; Coras gives it as the ground of what follows.]
- {a} (p087): **Acts 15 (15:8, "God, which knoweth the hearts"); 1 Thessalonians 2 (2:4, "God, which trieth our hearts")** — Actes c. xv la j des Theſſaloniſsiens. c. ij. [God as the searcher of hearts.]
- {b} (p087): **Jeremiah 23 (23:24: can any hide himself in secret places that I shall not see him?); Ecclesiasticus 16 (16:17–21, Vulgate: the eye of God sees all) and 23 (23:19 Vulgate 23:28, as above); Matthew, Mark and Luke as printed (chapters v, iij and ij): the saying that nothing is hidden that shall not be revealed is at Matthew 10:26, Mark 4:22 and Luke 8:17 and 12:2; Coras's chapter numbers do not match the modern ones and are given as printed** — Ieremie c. xxiij. Eccleſiaſtique xvj. & xxiij. S. Matthieu v. S. Marc iij. S. Luc ij. [The scriptural warrant that God brings every hidden thing to light.]
