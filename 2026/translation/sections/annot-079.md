⟦p106⟧This man said, with Propertius:

⟦p107⟧I am not moved by tears: I have been caught by that art before. You are always weeping, Cynthia, to lay a snare.

And in truth, because women seem to be born to weep, and shed tears when it pleases them and seems good to them {b},

They learn to weep becomingly, and they cry at whatever time they wish, and in whatever manner {c}.

One must not at all give credence to their tears, most often feigned, simulated and full of hypocrisy, nor to their words either: for women have (says Plautus) honey on their tongues; but their heart is all covered with gall, sprinkled with vinegar, full of every bitterness {d}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. LXXVIIII. on p106 (the print's numbering runs one ahead of the section id; see texte-76). The marker alphabet is *b*, *c*, *d*: the *a* of this annotation is the Propertius note printed in the margin of p106 and keyed *a2* by the transcription, which attaches it to texte-76 as an orphan (see there). The two Latin couplets are set by the print as separate paragraphs and are kept so; each is translated in the text and the Latin given below, since each runs to two lines. The `⟨alt⟩` at *cou uert⟨alt:uet⟩* is taken as *couuert* ("covered"); *couuet* is no word. Reflow gap read through: *cou uert*. Wrong sorts and slips translated by sense: *capus* for *captus* in the Propertius; *l'armoyent* for *larmoyent*; *a leus paroles* for *à leurs paroles*; in the margin *Tuſcul.* for *Trucul.* (note *d*). *Ceſtuy* → "This man" (Martin Guerre); *nées pour plourer* → "born to weep"; *feintes, ſimulees, & pleines d'hypocriſie* → "feigned, simulated and full of hypocrisy"; *fiel* → "gall"; *arrouſé de vinaigre* → "sprinkled with vinegar"; *amertume* → "bitterness".

- Verse (p107): **Propertius, *Elegies* 3.25.5–6** — Nil moueor lachrymis, iſta ſum capus ab arte. Semper ab inſidiis, Cynthia flere ſoles. [*Nil moveor lacrimis: ista sum captus ab arte; / semper ab insidiis, Cynthia, flere soles* — the poet's farewell to Cynthia, the last elegy of book 3, as the orphan note *a2* on p106 says.]
- {b} (p107): **Euripides, *Medea*, line 928** — Euripides in Medea. [Jason to Medea: a woman is a female thing and born to tears (γυνὴ δὲ θῆλυ κἀπὶ δακρύοις ἔφυ).]
- {c} (p107): **Ovid, *Ars amatoria*, book 3, lines 291–292** — Ouid. au iij de arte amãd. [*Quo non ars penetrat? discunt lacrimare decenter, / quoque volunt plorant tempore quoque modo*; the print's *Quóq;* is *quoque* with the *-que* sign. The `⟨alt⟩` *iij⟨alt:iiij⟩* is taken as *iij*: the lines are in book 3, and the *Ars* has only three books.]
- {d} (p107): **Plautus, *Truculentus*, lines 178–179** — Plaute⟨alt:Plaure⟩ en ſon Tuſcul. [*In melle sunt linguae sitae vostrae atque orationes, / facta atque corda in felle sunt sita atque acerbo aceto*. The print's *Tuſcul.* is a misprint for *Trucul.*; the `⟨alt⟩` is taken as *Plaute* (Plautus), *Plaure* being no name. In Plautus the lines are spoken by the maid Astaphium of men's tongues and hearts; Coras turns them on women.]
