⟦p058⟧On the contrary, that the prisoner was truly Martin Guerre, there were thirty or forty witnesses; among whom were the four sisters of the said Martin, who affirmed it, and rendered reasons for it ⟦p059⟧good and weighty, as having known, haunted and frequented him from his earliest years; eaten and drunk many times with him; and the sisters, for having always been brought up together.

## Notes
The print heads this section TEXTE (without a point, in spaced capitals) on p058. The page break falls between *raiſons* and *bonnes* and is kept at the corresponding words ("reasons for it ⟦p059⟧good and weighty"). Reflow gaps read through: *cõ gnu* = *cõgnu* (*cogneu*, known), *nour ries* = *nourries*. The print's point after *teſmoins* ("teſmoins. entre leſquels") is rendered by a semicolon, since the sentence continues. *Hanté & frequenté* → "haunted and frequented" as at texte-25; *rendoyent raiſons* → "rendered reasons" (the witness's *causa scientiae*, cf. annot-028's *rendre raison de son dire*); *bonnes & grandes* → "good and weighty"; *nourries touſiours enſemble* → "always brought up together" (*nourrir* in the sixteenth-century sense of rearing).
