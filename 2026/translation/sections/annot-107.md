⟦p150⟧This was a great conjecture, as we have represented above {a}, against the said du Tilh: inasmuch as of one who has been bad and wicked in the past there is great occasion to presume that he is still such, and perseveres in like malice {b}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOT. CVII. on p150; the transcription numbers it 106 (the printed numbering runs one ahead, flagged uncertain). The body carries two markers, {a} and {b}, both served by keyed notes. One `⟨alt⟩` in the margin of {a} (punctuation only), recorded in the batch report. *Vne grande coniecture* → "a great conjecture" per the glossary (*conjecture*); *remonſtré* → "represented" (*remonstrer* → to represent, texte-14); *mauuais & meſchant* → "bad and wicked" (*meschant* → wicked); *y a grande occaſion de preſumer* → "there is great occasion to presume"; *perſeuere en ſemblable malice* → "perseveres in like malice". The rule restates annot-037 (*en celuy qui vne fois a eſté mauuais… la loy… le preſume eſtre touſiours tel*), which Coras's margin names, and the same regula *Semel malus* is cited there at {c}.

- {a} (p150): **Coras, Annotation XXXVII (annot-037, p058), on the presumption that one once wicked remains so** — En l'annotatiõ xxxvij, [At p058 the printed and the counted numbers still agree.]
- {b} (p150): **Liber Sextus, *De regulis iuris*, regula 8 (*Semel malus semper praesumitur esse malus*)** — c. ſemel malus. de reg. iur. [Once bad, always presumed bad — the maxim of the Sext, cited without *in VI*; the civil-law title D. 50.17 has no such rule.]
