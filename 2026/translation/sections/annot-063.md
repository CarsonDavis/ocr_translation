⟦p084⟧At the beginning of this discourse several examples of likenesses were recited {a}; besides which, since the subject presents itself, Gnaeus Pompeius, afterwards called Strabo, father of the great Pompey, had a cook named Menogenes, who, because he squinted, was called by the surname Strabo, and who so vividly resembled his master that in the end the master, by the voice of the people, took on the surname of his cook, and was called Gnaeus Pompeius Strabo, that is to say, the Squinter {b}. Likewise we read also that Publius Cornelius Lentulus and Quintus Metellus Nepos, being Consuls of Rome in the year 697 from the founding of Rome, were so like two players of comedy — of whom the one was named Spinther, resembling Lentulus, and the other Pamphilus, resembling Metellus — that in the end Lentulus was surnamed Spinther; and Metellus likewise would have taken the surname of Pamphilus, had the surname of Pius not been given him beforehand {c}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. LXII. on p084. The marker alphabet restarts at *a*; the body carries {a}, {b} and {c}, all on p084, each served by a keyed note. Reflow gap read through: *anno tation*. The print's full stop after {a} ("des ſimilitudes {a}. outre leſquels") is rendered by a semicolon, and its lowercase *c'eſt à dire* after "Strabõ." is run on with a comma, since the sentence continues. *Au commencement de ce diſcours* → "At the beginning of this discourse" — Annotation V (p011–p020), where the doubles are catalogued; *Cn. Pompee* → "Gnaeus Pompeius" (the praenomen expanded; the father is Cn. Pompeius Strabo, consul 89 BC); *cuyſinier* → "cook"; *louſche* → "squinted"; *de ſurnom* → "by the surname"; *ſi viuement reſſemblant* → "so vividly resembled"; *par la voix du peuple* → "by the voice of the people"; *apporta le ſurnom* → "took on the surname"; *le Bigle* → "the Squinter" (Latin *strabo*); *en l'annee 697. de Rome baſtie* → "in the year 697 from the founding of Rome" (= 57 BC, the consulship of P. Cornelius Lentulus Spinther and Q. Caecilius Metellus Nepos — the date is right); *iouëurs de comedie* → "players of comedy" (Pliny has Spinther a *secundarum partium* actor and Pamphilus a *tertiarum*); *Spynther* → Spinther; *Pamphyle* → Pamphilus; *Metelle* → Metellus; *Pie* → Pius. Coras's remark that Metellus already bore the surname Pius follows his source as he read it; historically the cognomen Pius belonged to Q. Metellus Pius (consul 80 BC), not to Nepos, and the point is left as Coras gives it. The story of Pompey's father and the cook is the one promised at annot-060 {b}.

- {a} (p084): **Coras's own Annotation V (p011–p020)** — En l'anno tation v. [The catalogue of doubles: Vibius and Publicius, Scipio and Serapion, Hybreas, Toranius's twins, Sura's fisherman, and the rest.]
- {b} (p084): **Pliny, *Natural History* VII.10 (§54; c. 12 in the old chapter division)** — Pline au liu. vij. c. xij. [Pliny: Pompey's father exchanged his name for that of his cook Menogenes, whose squint he shared; cf. Valerius Maximus IX.14.2.]
- {c} (p084): **Pliny, *Natural History* VII.10 (§54) again, as printed** — Pline aud. lib. vij. c. xij. [*Aud.* = *au d[it]*, "in the said book". Pliny: Lentulus and Metellus Nepos, consuls together, were surnamed from the actors Spinther and Pamphilus; cf. Valerius Maximus IX.14.4.]
