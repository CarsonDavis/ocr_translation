⟦p160⟧The said du Tilh, heard once again, persevered in what is above, as many as three and four times; nay, even being on the ladder of the gibbet, before the house of the said Martin Guerre, where the execution was carried out, he confessed frankly to having built and carried out the said imposture, in the form above: asking pardon of the said Martin Guerre and Bertrande de Rols, married persons, and of the said Pierre Guerre, uncle of the said Martin, with great signs of repentance and detestation of his deed, crying all the while upon God for mercy, through His Son Jesus Christ. And thus he was executed, his body hanged, and afterwards burnt.

A RAISON CEDE.

Printed at Paris, by Fleury Prevost 1571.

## Notes
The print heads this section TEXTE. on p160, after Annotation CXI. (the 110th by the true count); it is the last section of the book, closing the record of the execution of 16 September 1560 that began at texte-100, and it is followed by the two display lines that end the volume: Coras's motto *A RAISON CEDE.* and the printer's colophon. The body carries no marker; the one margin note on p160, keyed *y*, is an orphan and is listed below at its place. Reflow gaps read through: *qua tre* (*quatre*), *tou ſiours* (*touſiours*). The print's full stop after *fois.* before *voire* is run on as a semicolon. *De rechef ouy ledit du Tilh* → "The said du Tilh, heard once again" (*ouy* → heard, of an accused, per §5); *perſeuere en ce que deſſus* → "persevered in what is above" (the record's *ce que deſſus* kept literal, as the preceding sections have it); *iuſques à trois & quatre fois* → "as many as three and four times"; *voire encore* → "nay, even"; *l'eſchelle du gibet* → "the ladder of the gibbet" (*gibet* → gibbet per the glossary; the condemned mounted a ladder set against the gallows and was pushed from it); *où l'execution fut faite* → "where the execution was carried out"; *confeſſa franchement* → "confessed frankly"; *auoir baſtie & executee ladite impoſture* → "to having built and carried out the said imposture" (Coras's building metaphor kept; *executee* here "carried out", not "executed", to keep the word for the hanging); *en la forme que deſſus* → "in the form above"; *mariez* → "married persons", as the decision has it at texte-80 (*Martin Guerre, & de Rols mariez*); *oncle dudit Martin* → "uncle of the said Martin", as at texte-80; *grans ſignes de repentance, & deteſtation de ſon faict* → "great signs of repentance and detestation of his deed"; *criant touſiours à Dieu miſericorde, par ſon fils Ieſus Chriſt* → "crying all the while upon God for mercy, through His Son Jesus Christ", the wording the case file §2 already uses; *Et ainſi fut executé, ſon corps pendu, & apres bruſlé* → "And thus he was executed, his body hanged, and afterwards burnt" (*bruſlé* → burnt as at texte-80; the decision ordered him *pendu, & eſtranglé … & apres ſon corps bruſlé*). *A RAISON CEDE.* is left in French per the case file §1: it is Coras's motto and printer's device, an anagram of IEAN DE CORAS ("yield to reason"), and Ringold and Lewis likewise left it untranslated. The colophon *Imprimé à Paris, par Fleury Preuoſt 1571.* → "Printed at Paris, by Fleury Prevost 1571." — Fleury Prévost, the Paris printer who printed the book for the publisher Galliot du Pré (*pour Galliot du Pré* on the title page); the colophon's 1571 stands a year before the title page's 1572, as the sheets were printed before the title was dated; the print's forms are kept.
