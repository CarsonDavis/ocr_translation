⟦p065⟧In the third place, the said de Rols, who has so virtuously prosecuted the said accused, when she was confronted with the said du ⟦p066⟧Tilh the prisoner (who was willing to believe her upon her oath, submitting himself to a thousand cruel deaths if she swore that he was not Martin Guerre her husband), never dared to swear; but said, bluntly enough, that she would not swear, nor believe him upon it either. Wherein the fraud and the calumny of the said de Rols could not be more patently discovered.

## Notes
The print heads this section TEXTE. on p065, after Annotation XLIII; the section carries no marginal citations. The page break falls between *du* and *Tilh* and is kept there ("the said du ⟦p066⟧Tilh"). Wrong sorts and slips translated by sense: *preenu* for *preuenu*; *ſe ſuſmettãt* for *ſe ſoubmettãt*; the print opens the parenthesis at *(qui l'en voulut croire* and opens a second at *(n'oſa iamais iurer* where it should close the first, rendered by a single closing parenthesis. Reflow gap read through: *calom nie*. *Si vertueuſement pourſuyui* → "so virtuously prosecuted" (the prisoner's irony, as at texte-14 *calomnieusement poursuyvi*); *preuenu* → "the accused" per §5; *confrontee* → "confronted" per §5; *l'en voulut croire à ſon ſerment* → "was willing to believe her upon her oath" (he offered to abide by her oath — the *delatio iurisiurandi* Annotation XLIV explains); *ſe ſoubmettant à mille morts cruelles* → "submitting himself to a thousand cruel deaths"; *aſſez creuëment* → "bluntly enough" (*crûment*); *ni l'en croire auſſi* → "nor believe him upon it either" (nor accept his oath in turn); *patemment* → "patently"; *fraude* and *calomnie* → "fraud" and "calumny" per the glossary. This is the third of the prisoner's five points (texte-40, texte-41, here, texte-43, texte-45).
