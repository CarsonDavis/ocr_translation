⟦p030⟧If the laws have found it bad to suborn and corrupt a slave (who is esteemed less than nothing, and as a dead person {a}) in order to lead him astray and turn him ⟦p031⟧from the service of his master {b}, with much greater reason [have they found it bad] to spoil and seduce, by gifts, presents, blandishments, promises and other such enticements, a free and freeborn person, and above all one so closely joined as a wife or a child {c}, dearer beyond comparison to the husband or to the father than all the most precious things in the world {d}.

## Notes
- {a} (p030): **Digest 50.17.32 (*De diversis regulis iuris antiqui*, l. *Quod attinet*) and Digest 50.17.209 (l. *Servitutem*)** — l, quod attinet. ſeruitutẽ. D. de reg. iur. [Ulpian: as regards the civil law, slaves are held as nobodies; and: we almost compare slavery to death.]
- {b} (p031): **Digest 11.3.1 (*De servo corrupto*, l. 1) and the other laws of the same title** — l. j. & autres au meſme rittre. D. de ſer. cor. [The praetor's edict giving an action in double against one who harbours another's slave or persuades him to his harm; *rittre* = *titre*.]
- {c} (p031): **Code 9.20 (*Ad legem Faviam de plagiariis*), l. *Si* (fragment not identified; the print's "Falc." is a slip for the lex *Favia* or *Fabia*, since the lex Falcidia concerns legacies); Digest 11.3 (*De servo corrupto*), l. *Et tantum*, § 1 (fragment not identified; the title's extension of the action to a son or daughter in power is the point)** — l. ſi. C. ad I. Falc. de plag. l. & tantum P. j. D. de ſcr. cer.
- {d} (p031): **Digest 4.2 (*Quod metus causa gestum erit*), l. *Isti quidem* (cf. D. 4.2.8, Paul; number unverified)** — l. iſti quidem. D. quod met. can. [Cited for the rule that fear inflicted through one's children counts as fear to oneself, children being dearer to a parent than anything.]
