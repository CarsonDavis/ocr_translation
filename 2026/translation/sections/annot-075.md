⟦p100⟧To understand whether this fault was excusable in the said de Rols, it must be presupposed that, as all other human actions are either voluntary or involuntary {a}, so crimes too are committed either voluntarily or not voluntarily {b}. Crimes voluntarily done are those which are executed with a deliberate purpose of committing them {c}, as to kill a man by lying in wait {d2}, to rape a woman, to steal, to bear false witness, and the like things, which are not committed without fraud and evil intention {e}: and after the execution they are unpardonable and irremissible as regards men {f}, even though he who has committed the act afterwards repents of it {g}. Otherwise, no one would ever be punished. For who is he that, to avoid death, corporal penalty, or ignominy, would not say, "I repent"? Those crimes also may be said to be committed voluntarily which are executed through some anger and sudden passion, as if I should kill him whom I find abusing my wife {h}, or who sets himself to kill me {i}.

⟦p101⟧And this kind of fault, although at the execution there was some will, caused by this or a like passion, nevertheless, because such will was not premeditated nor deliberate, but rather forced and constrained by passion {k}, is easily pardoned {l}, especially since it is very difficult for a man so justly provoked to restrain and master himself {m} — witness him who dared to lay hand upon Pope John XIII and cut his throat, having found him misconducting himself with his wife {n}. Crimes not voluntarily committed are those which are excused as fortuitous, and by some disaster of error or ignorance {o}: as when Telegonus, son of Ulysses and of Circe, by chance killed his father, not knowing him, and thinking he had to do with the servants, who would not allow him entry into his father's house {p}. As likewise if, at the hunt, thinking to hurl the dart against a roe-deer, a boar, or other wild beast, one should meet with a man who died or was wounded by that blow {q}. And in the deed which here presents itself: if a woman, thinking she has to do with her husband, is known by another, or a man, believing he approaches his wife, converses with another, both are more worthy of excuse than of penalty {r}, as we shall discourse more amply hereafter. Otherwise Lot would have been incestuous when he got his two daughters with child, believing he had to do with his wife {ſ}. Certainly such kinds of offences, inasmuch as they proceed from no evil will, seem to be excusable {t}; unless there were some preceding evil work which had occasioned this deed {u}: for already he who committed the crime was in fault, exercising an act in itself evil and reprobate, whence his intention, considering the beginning, was corrupted and depraved. As for example: if I have the will to murder Antoine, and, thinking to slay him, I kill François, I am not excused: for my purpose has always been to kill a man {x}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. LXXV. on p100; the transcription numbers it 74 (the printed numbering runs one ahead, flagged uncertain). The body carries twenty-one markers: {a}, {b}, {c}, {d2}, {e}, {f}, {g}, {h}, {i} on p100, and {k}, {l}, {m}, {n}, {o}, {p}, {q}, {r}, {ſ}, {t}, {u}, {x} on p101 — the usual alphabet without *j*, *v*, *w*, and with long *ſ* as a key between *r* and *t*; every one is served by a keyed note. The fourth note is printed *d*; since p100 already carries Annotation LXXIIII's note *d*, the transcription keys it *d2*, and the marker is given so here. The page break falls at the paragraph break before *Et telle maniere de coulpe* and is kept there. Reflow gaps read through: *hõ me*, *cor porelle*, *ſerui teurs*, *diſ courrons*, *of fenſes*, *oc caſionné*. Wrong sorts and slips translated by sense: *volontoiremeut* for *volontairement*, *commestẽt* for *commettent*, *qni* for *qui*, *aurre* for *autre*, *a faire* for *affaire*, *corrumpue* for *corrompue*. The print's stops after *ou fut bleſſé {q}.* and *d'vn autre.*, where the sentence runs on, are rendered by lighter punctuation. Six `⟨alt⟩` markers fall in this section (five in the body, three in the margin); the choices are in the batch report. *Faute* → "fault" (as at texte-72); *excuſable à l'endroit de* → "excusable in"; *volontaires ou inuolontaires* → "voluntary or involuntary"; *à propos deliberé* → "with a deliberate purpose" (cf. *propos deliberé & intention de malfaire* → deliberate purpose and intention to do wrong, the glossary's *dolus*); *tuer vn hõme de guet à pens* → "to kill a man by lying in wait" (*guet-apens*, the premeditated ambush; cf. *guetter & assaillir* → to lie in wait for and assail); *violer vne femme* → "to rape a woman"; *deſrober* → "to steal"; *porter faux teſmoignage* → "to bear false witness"; *ſans dol, & mauuaiſe intention* → "without fraud and evil intention" (*dol*, the Roman *dolus*); *imperdonnables, & irremiſſibles quãt aux hommes* → "unpardonable and irremissible as regards men" (as against God, who may remit them); *peine corporelle* → "corporal penalty"; *ignominie* → "ignominy"; *colere, & ſoudaine paſſion* → "anger and sudden passion"; *abuſant de ma femme* → "abusing my wife" (*abuser* → to abuse, per the glossary); *ſe met au deuoir de me tuer* → "sets himself to kill me"; *coulpe* → "fault" (the Latin *culpa*; the same English as *faute*, the French having both); *pour penſee, ni deliberee* → "premeditated nor deliberate" (*pourpensée*, cf. *malice pourpensee* → premeditated malice); *forcee & cõtrainte de paſſion* → "forced and constrained by passion"; *ſe pardonne aiſement* → "is easily pardoned"; *ſi iuſtement irrité* → "so justly provoked"; *ſe retenir, & dompter ſoy-meſmes* → "to restrain and master himself"; *le Pape Iean xiij* → "Pope John XIII" as printed — Platina's John XIII is the John XII (Octavian, 955–964) of the modern count, said to have been struck down by a jealous husband while in adultery; the form is kept as Coras and his source give it; *maluerſant auec ſa femme* → "misconducting himself with his wife" (cf. *mal-verser* → to go astray, of a wife); *fortuitement* → "as fortuitous"; *par quelque deſaſtre d'erreur ou d'ignorance* → "by some disaster of error or ignorance"; *Telegonus fils d'Vliſſes, & de Circé* → Telegonus, son of Ulysses and of Circe; *caſuellement* → "by chance" (the Digest's *casu*); *penſant auoir affaire aux ſeruiteurs* → "thinking he had to do with the servants"; *eſlancer le dart* → "to hurl the dart"; *cheureul, ſanglier* → "roe-deer, boar"; *au faict, qui ſe preſente* → "in the deed which here presents itself" (the case of Bertrande); *eſt cogneue d'vn autre* → "is known by another" (carnal knowledge, as *participer avec* → to have to do with); *conuerſe auec* → "converses with" (likewise carnal; the same verb at annot-077); *dignes plus d'excuſe, que de peine* → "more worthy of excuse than of penalty"; *inceſtueux* → "incestuous"; *engroſſit ſes deux filles* → "got his two daughters with child"; *œuure precedente mauuaiſe* → "preceding evil work" (the canonists' *versari in re illicita*); *en coulpe* → "in fault"; *acte de ſoy mauuais, & reprouué* → "act in itself evil and reprobate" (*reprouué* → reprobate, cf. *art reprouué*); *attendu le cõmencemẽt* → "considering the beginning"; *corrumpue & deprauee* → "corrupted and depraved"; *meurtrir* → "to murder", *occire* → "to slay". The stock names *Antoine*, *Françoys* are kept in French, as at annot-041.
