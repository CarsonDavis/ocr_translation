⟦p034⟧Great is the love of a wife toward her husband, when, to save his life, she offers herself to death, as of old did Alcestis, wife of Admetus, King of Thessaly, who, having learned by the oracle of Apollo that her husband, extremely sick and already brought to the last breaths of his life, could not recover health except by the voluntary death of one of his friends, and seeing that no one came forward to do it, she, inflamed with a great ardour and affection that boiled within her, exposed herself willingly to the precipice of death, to redeem the life of her husband {a}. Hypermnestra also, daughter of Danaus, put herself in hazard of being killed by her father, to save Lynceus her spouse, on whose death, and that of his other sons-in-law, to the number of fifty, Danaus with his fifty daughters had ⟦p035⟧conspired; of whom (says Horace {b}) there was found but one, Hypermnestra, who spared her consort and husband.

One among many, worthy of the nuptial torch, was splendidly false toward her perjured father, a maiden noble for all time.
