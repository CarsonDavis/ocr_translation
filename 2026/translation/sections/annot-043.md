⟦p065⟧The ancient proverbs of like things have most often been taken from eggs, or from milk, from water, or from honey-bees. Would you not have (says Cicero in some place) the proverb be true, of the likeness of eggs, so great that it is very hard to discern and tell apart the one from the other? And a little after: As eggs, says he, are like to eggs, and honey-bees among themselves {a}. And Sosia in Plautus, seeing Mercury to have taken his form, and to resemble him in everything, said: Milk is not more like to milk than that fellow is to me {b}. Messenio also, speaking to Menaechmus of Sosicles: Water, says he, is not so like to water, nor milk to milk, as Sosicles is to you, and you to him {c}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. XLIII. on p065. The marker alphabet restarts at *a* and runs a–c, all on p065; every note is keyed and every marker served. The print's *dit. il* (with a point) and *dit'il* (with an apostrophe) are both *dit-il*, "says he"; *ce lui-la* is *celui-la*. *Veux-tu pas … le prouerbe eſtre veritable* → "Would you not have … the proverb be true" (Cicero's question to his interlocutor); *entrecongnoiſtre* → "tell apart"; *le rapporter en tout* → "to resemble him in everything" (*rapporter* → "to take after" at annot-034); *mouſches à miel* → "honey-bees". The quotations are Coras's French renderings of the Latin and are translated from his French; the Latin is given in the notes. The names take their standard English forms: *Soſia* → Sosia (as at annot-003), *Menechmus* → Menaechmus, *Meſſenio* → Messenio; *Soſides* is Coras's form for Sosicles, the name of the second Menaechmus in Plautus, and is so rendered.

- {a} (p065): **Cicero, *Academica* II (*Lucullus*), §§ 54–57 (eggs as like as eggs and bees as bees, § 54; the proverbial likeness of eggs, § 57)** — Ciceron au ij. liure des Academiques. [Lucullus 57: *videsne ut in proverbio sit ovorum inter se similitudo?*; the argument is that the Stoics cannot tell twin eggs or bees apart, yet claim infallible perception.]
- {b} (p065): **Plautus, *Amphitryon*, line 601 (*neque lac lactis magis est simile quam ille ego similest mei*)** — Plaute en la premiere comedie, inſcripte Amphytrion. [Sosia, having met his double Mercury; "the first comedy" as at annot-003, *Amphitruo* standing first in the plays.]
- {c} (p065): **Plautus, *Menaechmi*, lines 1089–1090 (*neque aqua aquae nec lacte est lactis, crede mi, usquam similius, quam hic tui est, tuque huius autem*)** — Plaute en la comedie de Menechmus. [Messenio the slave, on the two Menaechmi; the twin of Syracuse is Menaechmus Sosicles, Coras's *Soſides*.]
