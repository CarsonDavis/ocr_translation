⟦p143⟧On this subject I have sometimes seen it called into doubt, to see whether a man who is going to die can be heard as a witness, or otherwise examined by a Judge: seeing that in this kind of hearings memory and recollection are singularly required {a}, which cannot well fall to one who, feeling the last breaths of his life draw near, and overcome by the hideous fright and horrible apprehension of death, is agitated and tormented in a thousand and a thousand ways {b}.

In a thousand forms of death one single death wearies the wretched (*Mille modis lethi miseros mors una fatigat*) {c}.

Witness our Redeemer Jesus Christ, who, foretasting the sharpness and bitterness of his dolorous passion, was sorrowful at it even unto death {d}, nay even to the point of grieving, and almost complaining, of God his father, saying, *Heli, Heli, lama sabathani?* My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me {e}? Nevertheless the contrary opinion is, with great reason, practised and received by all. For if he who is at the extremity of life can dispose of his goods by testament, sale, gift, and any other kind of disposition whatsoever, whether between the living or by reason of death {f} (in which, however, the integrity of a man's sense and understanding is greatly required {g}) — nay even, says the Emperor, though the dying man, crushed by sickness, were half dead and stammered with his tongue, provided that otherwise his conceptions and wishes be understood {h} — why is it that for the same reasons (where the case shall require it) the dying man cannot be heard and bear witness {i}? True it is that in this I desire the Judge, or Commissioner, who proceeds to the hearing to be prudent, wise, discreet and well advised, not to make the interrogations confused, long or prolix, but the clearest and briefest he can, comprising in few words the whole substance of the business {k}; inasmuch as, as has been said above, the spirit of him who feels near at hand the tearing asunder and separation of body and soul ⟦p144⟧(a thing which the pure philosophers have esteemed above all others horrible and frightful {l}, even to the wicked {m}), being infinitely travailed and afflicted, would be so still more if he found himself pressed to remember so many and so many things, about which he might be too curiously and prolixly searched {n}. For who is there (said Cicero) from whom, when death approaches, the blood does not withdraw, and who does not turn white with fright and fear?

Who is there, or how few are there, from whom, when death draws near, the timid blood does not flee back, and who does not grow pale with fear {o}?

And in some other place: Who is there (he says) who, being on the point of dying, has his Spirit tranquil and at rest {p}?

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. CI. on p143; the transcription numbers it 100. The first of the eleven annotations added in 1565 on the record of the execution (case file §1); it expounds *& illec fut ouy par ledit Iuge de Rieux* (annot-099, end) — whether a man about to die can be heard as a witness. Markers a–p (no *j*) on p143–p144, all served. One page break, at *de l'ame | (choſe*, kept at "body and soul ⟦p144⟧(a thing". Five `⟨alt⟩` markers, listed in the report; none changes the English. Reflow gaps read through: *dou te* (*doute*), *appro cher*, *l'hi deuſe* (*l'hideuſe*), *pre ſentant* (*preſentant*), *l'en tendement*, *pro chaine*. Three verse quotations: the Statius line, one line, translated in the text with the Latin in parentheses (the print's *modus* is *modis*); the Cicero couplet at {o}, two lines, translated in the text with the Latin in the Notes; the words from the cross, kept in Coras's Latinised Aramaic with his French rendering translated after them. *Audition* → "hearing" (texte-21); *enquis* → "examined"; *memoire & ſouuenance* → "memory and recollection"; *eſchoir* → "fall to"; *preſentant* → "foretasting" (*pressentir*); *contriſté iuſqu'à la mort* → "sorrowful … even unto death" (Matthew 26:38 in the older English); *ſe douloir* → "grieving"; *diſpoſer de ſes biens* → "dispose of his goods" (*biens* → goods, property); *donnaiſon* → "gift"; *entre vifs, ou à cauſe de mort* → "between the living or by reason of death" (*inter vivos*, *mortis causa*); *integrité du ſens, & de l'entendement* → "integrity of … sense and understanding" (D. 28.1.2's *integritas mentis*); *moribunde* → "dying man"; *begueyaſt de ſa langue* → "stammered with his tongue"; *conceptions & volontez* → "conceptions and wishes"; *Commiſſaire* → "Commissioner" (annot-021); *negoce* → "business"; *diuulſion* → "tearing asunder"; *trauaillé* → "travailed"; *curieuſement* → "curiously" (in the old sense, minutely).
