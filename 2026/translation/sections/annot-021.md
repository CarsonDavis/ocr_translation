⟦p039⟧These matters here discoursed at length, and the multitude of so many and so many tokens so true, gave the Judges great occasion to persuade themselves of the innocence of the said du Tilh, and moreover to wonder at the happiness and felicity of his memory, which had known how to recite innumerable things done and past more than twenty years ago; wherein the commissioners, who by all means possible to them strove to catch him in some lie, could nevertheless gain nothing upon him, nor prevent him from answering truly to all things, on which nevertheless he was by them separately and at intervals interrogated. Which drew the judges more and more into wonder, who, for the great felicity of so happy a memory, would gladly have compared him to a Scipio, Cyrus, Theodectes, Mithridates, Themistocles, Cineas, Metrodorus or Lucullus, persons excellent in the gift of memory, and eternally celebrated {a}, if the wretched end of this prodigious cheat had not offended the splendour of such and so well-marked persons, by comparing to them so impudent, deplorable and wretched a man {b}. Certainly, if one could without scruple make use of such comparisons, it would be very apt with Porcius Latro, the great companion of Seneca, who boasted that he had never been deceived by his memory in a single word; and, to make trial of it, would often have proposed to him the name of some ancient captain or other illustrious and renowned person, at the pleasure of him who named him, of whom on the spot he would recite from top to bottom the pure truth of all the deeds since his ⟦p040⟧childhood, together with the words he had spoken, without ever failing by a nail's breadth {e}; as our rustic likewise did of all the acts and words of the said Martin.
