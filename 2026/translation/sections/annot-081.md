⟦p110⟧The crime of forgery is here patent: by the change of name and the substitution of the person, the said du Tilh having asserted that he was called, and was, Martin Guerre: for although it is lawful for everyone to change his name at his pleasure {a2}, inasmuch as names are imposed only to recognise persons and to distinguish them one from another {b2}: this nevertheless is understood with good faith, and without intention to defraud or injure another {c2}. As when Pope Sergius, the second of that name, who was called in Italian Bocca di porco, that is to say Pig's Mouth, had himself named Sergius: whence since then the Pontiffs of Rome have drawn the custom of leaving, at their creation, their proper name, to take another — after the example also (as they say) of Jesus Christ, who gave the name of Peter to Simon Bar-Jona when he chose him for his disciple. {d2} Provided therefore that the intention be not evil, one may change not only one's name, but also one's surname and arms {e2}, and call oneself of another country: as when St Paul, in the Acts of the Apostles, called himself a citizen of Rome, although he was none {f2}. And Jesus Christ, speaking ⟦p111⟧of St John, said that he was Elijah {g}, whom the Lord God had promised by the Prophet {h}: not, says St Augustine, that Jesus understood St John to be the very person of Elijah, but by imitation of virtue {i}. Otherwise, whoever changes his name must be punished and repressed by the penalty of forgery {x}, and such a one (said the Jurisconsult Ulpian in some place) that he may be an example to others {l} [unclear: the print reads *& telle, (diſoit en quelque lieu le Iuriſconſulte Vlpien qu'il en ſoit) autres aux exemple*, with the parenthesis closed too late and *autres aux* inverted; translated by the evident sense].

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOT. LXXXI. on p110 (the print's numbering runs one ahead of the section id). The marker alphabet is keyed *a2*–*f2* on p110 by the transcription, since p110 already carries Annotation LXXX's notes *a*–*d*; the print has plain letters, and the markers are kept as the transcription keys them. On p111 the run continues *g*, *h*, *i*, then the marker the transcription reads *x* (with `⟨alt:{k}⟩`), then *l*. The margin of p111 keys its note *x* likewise, so *x* is taken for the body marker to keep body and margin matched, though the alphabet expects *k*; the `⟨alt⟩` choice is recorded in the report. The margin of p110 also carries an unkeyed note, *ſiue adulterium. x. diſt.*, which the transcription marks an orphan; it is listed below without a marker. The `⟨alt⟩` at *bonne foy,⟨alt:foy;⟩* is taken with the comma (the clause continues *& ſans intention*); at *d'ar⟨alt:d'ar-⟩ moiries* with the hyphen (*d'armoiries* is one word broken at the line end); in the margin *l'a notarion,⟨alt:notation,⟩* is taken as *notation* (*l'annotation*, a cross-reference; *anotarion* is no word), *iij⟨alt:iiij⟩* in note *d2* is kept as *iij* for want of any basis to prefer the other (unverified, see the report), *x.⟨alt:x,⟩* as the full stop closing the citation, and *xi⟨alt:xi.⟩* in note *g* is indifferent. Reflow gaps read through: *laiſ ſer*, *ſu nom*, *d'ar moiries*, *per ſonne*, *pei ne*. Wrong sorts and slips translated by sense: *ou* for *où* in *frauder, ou endommager* (here *ou* "or" is right); *c'eſt a dire* without grave; *Prophe* for *Prophete*; *diſoit … qu'il en ſoit) autres aux exemple*, the disordered close of the annotation, marked `[unclear]`; in the margin *mura no.* for *muta. no.* (note *a2*), *pralaror* for *prælator.* (note *e2*). The full stop before {d2} and the one after {x} are the print's. *Le crime de faux* → "The crime of forgery" per the glossary (annot-012); *patent* → "patent"; *changement du nom, & ſuppoſition de la perſonne* → "the change of name and the substitution of the person"; *aſſeuré* → "asserted"; *loiſible* → "lawful"; *diſcerner les vnes des autres* → "to distinguish them one from another"; *bonne foy* → "good faith"; *frauder, ou endommager autruy* → "to defraud or injure another"; *Bocca di porco* kept in Italian, Coras's French gloss *Bouche de pourceau* → "Pig's Mouth"; *à leur creation* → "at their creation" (their election); *Simon Bariona* → "Simon Bar-Jona" (Matthew 16:17); *la volonté ne ſoit mauuaiſe* → "the intention be not evil"; *ſurnom* → "surname"; *armoiries* → "arms"; *ſe dire d'vn autre pays* → "call oneself of another country"; *bien qu'il n'en fut point* → "although he was none" (Coras's view that Paul, of Tarsus, was no citizen of the city of Rome; translated as he wrote it); *Elie* → "Elijah"; *par imitation de vertu* → "by imitation of virtue"; *puni & reprimé par peine de faux* → "punished and repressed by the penalty of forgery"; *le Iuriſconſulte Vlpien* → "the Jurisconsult Ulpian" per the glossary (*Jurisconsultes*). Coras told the same doctrine of names in Annotation XII (annot-012), to which note *c2* refers; the Sergius story (the pope called *Os porci* who took a new name) is told by Platina and Crinito of Sergius II (844–847), though later writers attach it to Sergius IV; translated as printed.
