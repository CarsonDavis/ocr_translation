⟦p068⟧Fifthly, the accused having been, for other deeds, made prisoner by authority of the Seneschal of Toulouse, and at the instance of Jean d'Escornebœuf, called the Cadet — the said Pierre Guerre always underhand supplying every favour and aid — this deed too was advanced against him; whereof the same de Rols complained incessantly against the said Pierre Guerre and his wife, who wished to constrain her to accuse the same accused, being resolved to have him put to death, or at the least to have him put in the Galleys. And when he had come out of prison, by virtue of the interlocutory order upon contrary facts [*appointement de contraires*: an order admitting the parties to prove their contrary allegations] given by the said Seneschal, being back at Artigat, the said de Rols received him and made much of him as her husband; and as soon as he had arrived, gave him a white shirt, nay, washed his feet, and afterwards they lay together.

## Notes
The print heads this section TEXTE. on p068, after Annotation XLVI; the section carries no marginal citations. The `⟨alt?:⟩` at *commẽ⟨alt?:comme⟩ mari* is unconfirmed by the transcribers on either side; both readings give *comme* ("as"), and the sense is the same either way; *comme* is taken. Wrong sorts and slips translated by sense: *apointement* for *appointement*; *leſd.* is the print's abbreviation of *leſdits*. Reflow gaps read through: *Pier re*, *blã che*, *cou cherent*. *Pour autres faits* → "for other deeds" (Coras does not state the charge; the case file §2 notes the secondary literature gives it as arson, unverified); *conſtitué priſonnier* → "made prisoner" (cf. texte-46, *le fit conſtituer priſonnier*); *par authorité du Seneſchal de Toloſe* → "by authority of the Seneschal of Toulouse", the first-instance court distinct from the judge of Rieux and from the Parlement, per §4–5; *à la requeſte du Capdet Iean d'Eſcornebeuf* → "at the instance of Jean d'Escornebœuf, called the Cadet", the name and by-name in the case file's forms (§3, §8: *Cadet* a by-name for a younger son, not a rank; the print's *Capdet* is the Gascon form, and it spells the surname *Eſcornebeuf*); *à la requeſte de* is the phrase "at the instance of", not the written *requeste* → petition of the glossary; *ſouſminiſtrant touſiours par deſſouz main* → "always underhand supplying"; *on luy auança ce fait auſſi* → "this deed too was advanced against him" (brought up as a charge); *icelle de Rols* → "the same de Rols"; *inceſſamment* → "incessantly"; *deliberez de* → "being resolved to"; *faire mettre en Galere* → "to have him put in the Galleys" (an afflictive punishment, §5; Coras's capital kept); *appointement de contraires* → "interlocutory order upon contrary facts", glossed in brackets on this first use: *appointement* → interlocutory order per §5; *de contraires* is the *appointement en faits contraires*, which admits both parties to prove their contrary allegations and, here, let the prisoner go; *receut, & careſſa* → "received him and made much of him" per the glossary (*recevoir & caresser*, texte-17); *chemiſe blanche* → "a white shirt" (of linen, *blanche* means freshly laundered — a clean shirt; the literal word is kept); *voire luy laua les pieds* → "nay, washed his feet"; *coucherent enſemble* → "lay together". This is the fifth and last of the prisoner's points; the case file §2 and §10 record the foot-washing and the next morning's arrest, which texte-46 relates.
