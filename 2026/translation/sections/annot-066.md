⟦p085⟧Ulpian in a certain place, speaking of a legacy made by a testator on condition that the legatee should swear, says that such a condition must be rejected, and that the legatee may boldly demand the legacy without taking the oath with which the testator charged him: so that, says he, he may neither lose the legacy by being unwilling to swear, nor win it by perjuring himself. For there are some, says the Jurisconsult, too ready to swear, in contempt of religion; others fearful of the divine power even to superstition, so that even of things which they know and are well assured of, they nevertheless would not, nor would dare to, swear {a2}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. LXV. on p085. The body carries a single marker, {a2}, on p085, served by the one keyed note; the margin continues the second alphabet begun at Annotation LXIII. Reflow gaps read through: *har diment* (*hardiment*), *puiſſan ce* (*puiſſance*). The print's full stop after *gagne* ("il le gagne. car il en y a") is rendered by a full stop and a new sentence, as printed; its *de ſorte, que* keeps the comma's force as "so that". *Vlpian* → "Ulpian" (Domitius Ulpianus, the jurist of the Digest); *vn lais* → "a legacy" (*lais*, Old French for *legs*; and *legat* → "legacy", Latin *legatum*); *legataire* → "legatee"; *telle cõdition doit eſtre reiettée* → "such a condition must be rejected" (Ulpian: *a praetore reprobantur*, the praetor strikes it out); *hardiment* → "boldly"; *demander le legat* → "demand the legacy"; *duquel le teſtateur le chargeoit* → "with which the testator charged him"; *ne voulãt iurer, il ne perde le legat: ou ſe periurant, il le gagne* → "he may neither lose the legacy by being unwilling to swear, nor win it by perjuring himself"; *le Iuriſcõſulte* → "the Jurisconsult" per the glossary; *trop faciles à iurer, en meſpris de la religion* → "too ready to swear, in contempt of religion" (Ulpian: *facilitate iurandi contempta religione*); *craintifs de la puiſſance diuine, iuſqu'à ſuperſtition* → "fearful of the divine power even to superstition" (*perquam timidi metu divini numinis usque ad superstitionem*); *ils ne voudroient pourtant, ni oſeroient iurer* → "they nevertheless would not, nor would dare to, swear". The annotation is Coras's authority for the *Texte*'s point that a refusal to swear may spring from scruple, not from a guilty conscience.

- {a2} (p085): **Digest 28.7 (*De condicionibus institutionum*), l. *Quae sub condicione* (D. 28.7.8 pr.–§ 1, Ulpian)** — l. quæ ſub conditione. D de cond. inſt [*Quae sub condicione iurisiurandi relinquuntur, a praetore reprobantur*: dispositions left under condition of an oath are disallowed by the praetor, who provides that the beneficiary shall neither lose the inheritance or legacy by omitting the condition nor be compelled to swear shamefully; for some are too ready to swear in contempt of religion, others too timid through fear of the godhead, even to superstition — Coras's whole annotation is a paraphrase of this law.]
