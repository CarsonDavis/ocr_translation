⟦p129⟧A penalty, assuredly, as has been shown above, worthy of so detestable a lecher and heinous traitor {a3}. For hanging on the gibbet is, of all the punishments the ancients were able to devise, the most foul, ignominious, base and infamous: wherefore the Poets have called such a death base, filthy, ugly, unsightly and wretched. As Virgil, speaking of Amata, mother of Lavinia, who, indignant against her daughter, whom Aeneas had married (although she and King Latinus her father had pledged her to Turnus), hanged and strangled herself, saying {b2},

And she knots from a high beam the noose of an unsightly death (*Et nodum informis leti trabe nectit ab alta*).

Achaeus also, king of Lydia, because he overburdened his people with great tributes and insupportable charges, was, in a popular sedition, to the perpetual ignominy ⟦p130⟧of himself, of his posterity and of such tyrants, hanged with his feet uppermost and his head hanging over the river Pactolus, whose sand is of gold. Which Ovid eloquently expresses when he says {e}:

Or may you perish by the death of captive Achaeus, hanged, who, wretched, hung by a rope over the gold-bearing water (*Morte vel intereas, capti suspensus Achaei, Qui miser aurifera reste pependit aqua*).

And in the books of the Roman Pontiffs it was ordained, among other things, that the bodies of the hanged, as abominable, should have no burial, but should be, as in disdain, thrown upon the ground, to be eaten and devoured by birds, dogs and other famished beasts.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOT. XCIII. on p129; it expounds *eſtre pendu & eſtranglé* (texte-91). The body carries three markers, {a3}, {b2} and {e}: the transcription keys the first two *a3* and *b2* because p129 already carries the notes *a*, *a2* and *b* of Annotations XCI and XCII (the print has plain letters), and the third, on p130, is keyed *e* by the margin. At that third marker the transcription offers `{e}.⟨alt:{t}.⟩`; *e* is taken, since the margin's note is keyed *e* and no note *t* exists — the choice is recorded in the report. The other `⟨alt⟩` choices: *don[?]⟨alt:dont⟩* → *dont* ("wherefore"); *appelé⟨alt:app elé⟩* is the same word with or without the reflow gap; *vel⟨alt:Vel⟩* → *vel*, lowercase, as a word inside Ovid's verse. Reflow gaps read through: *pé dement* (*pendement*, the tilde of *pẽdement* lost), *par lant*, *con tre*, *Pacto lus*, *affa mées*. Wrong sorts and slips translated by sense: *prodireur* for *proditeur* ("traitor"); *mere des Lauinia* for *de Lauinia*; the parenthesis after *Eneas* is closed where none was opened, so the parentheses are placed around the *bien que* clause; *nondum* for Virgil's *nodum* ("noose"), translated as Virgil has it; *reſte* in the Ovid line (the manuscripts and editions read *teste*, "with the gold-bearing water as witness"), translated as printed, since *reste*, "by a rope", construes. The two verse quotations are translated in the text with the Latin in parentheses, and the print's setting of each as its own paragraph is kept. Coras's history is translated as he gives it: Virgil's Amata hangs herself on the report of Turnus's death, before Lavinia's marriage, and his Achaeus — the Seleucid pretender captured at Sardis in 213 BC, whose mutilated body Antiochus III hung up — is made by Coras "king of Lydia" and the victim of a popular rising. *Paillard* → "lecher" and *flagitieux* → "heinous" per the glossary; *excogiter* → "devise"; *ord* → "foul"; *vilain* → "base"; *informe* → "unsightly" (Virgil's *informis*); *malheureuſe* → "wretched"; *dediee à Turnus* → "pledged to Turnus"; *ſe pendit elle meſme, & eſtrangla* → "hanged and strangled herself" (the *arrest*'s pair); *importables charges* → "insupportable charges"; *ſeditiõ populaire* → "popular sedition"; *les pieds contremont* → "with his feet uppermost"; *diſertemẽt* → "eloquently"; *les liures des Pontifes Romains* → "the books of the Roman Pontiffs" (the pagan pontifical books, not the canon law — Coras's *loi des Pontifes* elsewhere means the latter); *comme par deſdain* → "as in disdain".
