⟦p046⟧And as to the Accused, there are about one hundred and fifty witnesses heard, of whom thirty or forty assure that he is truly Martin Guerre, for having seen, haunted and frequented him from his childhood, and recognise in him certain marks and scars which the said Martin had. Others, and in greater number, depose that it is Arnauld du Tilh, called Pansette, and for the same reasons, for having known him from the cradle; the rest of the witnesses, up to sixty and more, that there is so ⟦p047⟧great a likeness that they doubt, and would not dare to assure whether it is the one or the other. There are also made two summary inquiries [*sommaires aprises*] upon the resemblance of Sanxi Guerre, son of Martin, and of the sisters of the said Martin, with the accused; from which result two proofs very different, for by the first it is reported that Sanxi, son of Martin, does not resemble the accused at all, and by the second, that the sisters of this Martin strongly resemble the accused. The matter set down in law, by judgment the said du Tilh, prisoner, is condemned to lose his head and to be put in four quarters, and the judgment extended [*amplié l'arreſt*: her conclusions granted] in favour of the said de Rols. Whereof this du Tilh is appellant in the Court of the Parlement of Toulouse; which, using its accustomed providence, and considering the importance of this business, orders that Pierre Guerre, the uncle, and the said de Rols shall come in person. Afterwards they are confronted in full chamber with the said accused, first the wife; where the said du Tilh showed a countenance so assured, and much more so than the said de Rols, that there were few judges present who were not persuaded that the prisoner was the true husband, and the imposture proceeded from the side of the wife and of the uncle. Nevertheless the Court, being not yet by that sufficiently instructed, orders that inquiry be made on its own motion ⟦p048⟧upon certain facts, and other witnesses heard than those of the inquiries made by the first judge. But what! these inquiries by authority of the Court made, the judges were more uncertain than ever; for of twenty-five or thirty witnesses heard on the court's own motion, nine or ten assured that it was Martin Guerre, and seven or eight that it was Arnauld du Tilh, and the rest, for the conflict of the circumstances and of the likeness of the prisoner, doubted of it, not daring to assure that it was the one rather than the other.
