⟦p033⟧In former times, when some creditor wished to call his debtor into judgment, before entering into suit he would take him aside, and admonish and call upon him familiarly to pay and satisfy him, not once only, but most often twice and three times. Thus Proculus the Jurisconsult teaches us that, if our neighbour or another does us some wrong, [we ought] to speak to him and amicably represent it to him before bringing him to law. I WISH, says he, that you would speak with Hiberus, so that he may not do an unjust thing {c}. Acts not only humane and full of all civility, but also savouring of Christianity, by whose law we are commanded to correct our neighbour before threatening him {d}, and to take up a quarrel with no one, but to be humane, gracious and charitable, using all gentleness and courtesy toward men {e}; from whom, if we receive some wrong or injury, we must nevertheless not hold him who offends us as an enemy, but admonish him as a brother {f}, and reprove and correct him amicably between ourselves and him alone {g}.
