⟦p066⟧It is a great shame and baseness (said Paul the Jurisconsult), and a patent confession of the matter in question, to be willing neither to swear nor to tender the oath {a}; for even where all other proofs should fail {b}, nay, where there should be some presumption against him who tenders it, and he were charged with the proof, it would nevertheless be lawful for him, to cut [the matter] short and search out the truth, to tender the oath to his party {c}: who can have no just reason for not swearing, inasmuch as from a party he is made judge, by the will and consent of his adversary {d}. I say this, saving what our Interpreters discourse of it more amply in the common places [the titles where the subject is ordinarily treated], and what we shall write of it a little hereafter {e}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. XLIIII. on p066. The marker alphabet restarts at *a* and runs a–e, all on p066; every note is keyed and every marker served. Wrong sorts and slips translated by sense: *preſumptioo* for *preſumption*; in the margin *I. manife. ſta.* for *l. manifeſta* (note *a*) and *l fin.* without a point (note *c*); the print sets an italic capital *P* for the paragraph sign § in note *b*, and two points before the number in note *e* ("annotation.. xv."). Reflow gaps read through: *defe rer*, *licen tia*, *al leguee*. The print sets a point after {c} ("à ſa partie {c}. qui ne peut"), rendered by a colon, since the sentence continues. *Le Iuriſconſulte Paule* → "Paul the Jurisconsult" (Paulus, whose words in the Digest Coras paraphrases; cf. *Hermogenien Iuriſconſulte* at annot-038); *honte & vilennie* → "shame and baseness" (Paul's *turpitudo*); *patente confeſſion* → "patent confession"; *du faict duquel ſ'agit* → "of the matter in question"; *deferer le ſerment* → "to tender the oath" (the *delatio iurisiurandi*: a party offers to abide by his adversary's oath on the disputed fact; the adversary may swear, or refer the oath back; to refuse both is taken as confession); *où toutes autres preuues defaudroyent* → "where all other proofs should fail"; *chargé de la preuue* → "charged with the proof" (bearing the burden of proof); *pour couper & recercher la verité* → "to cut [the matter] short and search out the truth", the bracketed object supplied; *ſa partie* → "his party" (the opposing party, per the glossary *partie*); *il eſt fait iuge* → "he is made judge" (the party to whom the oath is tendered decides the cause by swearing); *nos Interpretes* → "our Interpreters" as at annot-039; *ès lieux communs* → "in the common places", glossed in brackets: the *loci communes*, the titles where the doctors treat the subject at large.

- {a} (p066): **Digest 12.2.38 (*De iureiurando sive voluntario sive necessario sive iudiciali*, l. *Manifestae*; Paul: *Manifestae turpitudinis et confessionis est nolle nec iurare nec iusiurandum referre*)** — I. manife. ſta. D. de iure iuran. [The print's *I.* is *l.*; the fragment is the text Coras paraphrases.]
- {b} (p066): **Code 6.30.22 (*De iure deliberandi et de adeunda vel adquirenda hereditate*; Justinian's law *Scimus*, the last of the title — the print's *l. ſi.* is *l. Sci[mus]*), § 10 (*Licentia danda creditoribus seu legatariis vel fideicommissariis … vel per sacramentum illius, si aliae probationes defecerint*: the heir's oath on the inventory where all other proofs fail); Digest 12.2.35 (*De iureiurando*, l. *Tutor pupilli*; Paul)** — l. ſi. P. licen tia. C. de iur. de lib. l. tutor pupilli. D. de iure iu. [The title *de iur. de lib.* is read as *de iure deliberandi*; the *P.* stands for the paragraph sign.]
- {c} (p066): **Code 6.42.32 (*De fideicommissis*), the last law of the title (Justinian, *Quaestionem ex facto emergentem…*)** — l fin. C. de fideicom. [The bearing of this law on the oath tendered by the party charged with the proof could not be verified.]
- {d} (p066): **The Doctors (commentators) on the law *Manifestae* already cited (at {a})** — Les Docteurs en la l. manifeſta al leguee. [The commonplace that he to whom the oath is tendered is made judge of the cause by the consent of his adversary.]
- {e} (p066): **Coras, Annotation XV as printed (cross-reference)** — En l'annotation.. xv. [Annotation XV treats the corruption of a slave and does not discuss the oath, and Coras says the discussion is to come "a little hereafter"; the number is probably a wrong sort for a later annotation, on Bertrande's refusal to swear. Given as printed.]
