⟦p079⟧One might here raise some difficulty, and think that one ought not to give great faith to the last deposition of these witnesses, who depart from the first: both because they have varied {a}, and thus, by the contradiction and repugnance of their depositions, themselves overthrow it {b}; and because also they are perjured, and the testimony of a perjurer, as everyone understands, ought to be rejected {c}; especially since, if any faith could be given to one of their depositions, it would have to be to the first, made with oath {d}. But all these difficulties can easily be resolved if one comes to consider that it is very reasonable that a witness who, through error and the circumvention of another, has deposed falsely should, as soon as his error appears to him, correct himself {e}: and thus not only on the spot, or shortly after (as is indubitably permitted him {f}), but even after a long space and interval of time, if until then he had not perceived his error, or else some other just cause newly presents itself {h}. The judgment of which, together with the distance of time, is wholly referred to the discretion of the judge {i}. Otherwise, if the witness, for his pleasure and of his own free will, long after his deposition wished to depart from it, and to change or correct what he had already deposed, he would not be received to do so, for fear of subornation {k}.
