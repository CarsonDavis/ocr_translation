⟦p158⟧To execute is nothing other than to effect, accomplish and finish something; whence executors, in our laws, are properly called those who bring to an end and effect the sentence of the judge {a2}. And by the same reason, in the matter of last wills, Executors are cal⟦p159⟧led those who have the charge of effecting and accomplishing the will of the deceased {b}; of whom the interpreters have been accustomed to make three kinds, namely Testamentary, Legitimate and Dative [*Donnez*, "given"]. Testamentary are those to whom the deceased, in his testament, has given the charge of effecting his will {c}, be they laymen or clerks, secular or regular; for even religious, with leave (however) of their superior, may be executors of testaments {d}, the Cordeliers alone excepted {e} — as to whom, moreover, some teach that a Guardian of Saint Francis, although he cannot be executor of a testament {f}, if nevertheless he has been left executor and has executed, no one opposing or gainsaying, the execution is valid {g}; inasmuch as this charge is not forbidden to the religious of this order for the defect or vice of their persons, but for the estate and perfection (as one says) of their rule {h}. And therefore, in like terms, priests and clerks in holy orders, to whom it is not permitted to exercise the office of tabellion or notary, if nevertheless they receive instruments, the parties not gainsaying, such instruments are good and valid {i}. Now these testamentary executors, because they are bound to render account and pay over the balance remaining {k}, must (after the example of guardians {l}), before all else, make an inventory of the goods of the deceased {m}, without which one could not afterwards recover from them accounts or the payment of the balance {n}. Legitimate executors are those to whom this charge and faculty is given by the law, as is the Bishop {o}, who must watch over and provide that the pious wills of the deceased be both faithfully and entirely accomplished {p}. And to these ends he must admonish, twice at the least, the heir or the testamentary executor (if there is any) to pay, or cause to be paid, the bequests made to the poor or to other pious works; and he who does not satisfy this on his part within the year shall lose all the profit which he could other⟦p160⟧wise gather from the testamentary disposition {r}. Which the bishop may do although the deceased had expressly forbidden the bishop to meddle in it {ſ}, inasmuch as such prohibitions are private [unclear: *priuées*; perhaps for *praves*, "perverse"] and little reasonable, nay, seem to contain ineptitude and some impiety {t}; wherefore they cannot hinder the force of equitable laws, the virtue of the holy constitutions {u}. And if the bishop were negligent in making the aforesaid admonitions and procuring the accomplishment of such wills, recourse must be had to the Metropolitan {x}. Dative executors are those whom the Judge, consul or other magistrate appoints, the heirs of the deceased having been called.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOT. CXI. on p158; by the true count it is the 110th (the print's numbers run one ahead from Annotation 84), the last of the hundred and eleven the title page promises, and it expounds the naming of the guardians as executors (texte-108). The margin alphabet runs a2–x on p158–p160 (no *j*, *v*, *w*; *ſ* as a key; the first note is keyed *a2* by the transcription because p158 already carries Annotation CX's second alphabet *a*–*d*; the body marker is the same and is kept). The body carries twenty markers, all served below; the margin note *q* (p159, the Novel and authentica on the heir who does not pay the legacies within the year) has no marker in the body and is listed as an orphan at its place (its matter is the sentence *celuy qui n'y ſatisfera … dans l'an*). Page breaks fall inside *appel|lez* ("cal⟦p159⟧led") and inside *autre|ment* ("other⟦p160⟧wise"), and are kept at the corresponding words. Reflow gaps read through: *ac couſtumé*, *execu té*, *recou urer*, *œu ures*, *im pieté*, *Don nez*; in the margin *Fe lin*, *al leguée*, *deſ ſus*, *admini ſtra*, *au tem*, *iuſsi*. Wrong sorts and slips translated by sense: *enmatiere* for *en matiere*; *on clercs* for *ou clercs*; *enſeigent* for *enſeignent*; *noraire* for *notaire*; *dipoſition* for *diſpoſition*; *accompliment*; *ſon ceux* for *ſont ceux*; in the margin *e. tua nobis* and *o. tua nobis* for *c. tua nobis* (notes *b*, *d*), *cdlla.* for *colla.* (note *o*), *Autene.* for *Autent.* (note *q*), *clem. cxiiij.* / *cxiij.* (notes *e*, *f*, *h*; see below). One `⟨alt⟩` in the body (*meinent* / *meïnent*), not affecting the English; see the report. *Executer / effectuer, accomplir, & acheuer* → "to execute / to effect, accomplish and finish"; *meinent à fin* → "bring to an end"; *la ſentence du iuge* → "the sentence of the judge" (the generic *sententia*, here of any judge; cf. the case file §5); *dernieres volontez* → "last wills"; *deffuncts* → "the deceased"; *Teſtamentaires, Legitimes & Donnez* → "Testamentary, Legitimate and Dative", Coras's *Donnez* glossed in brackets on first use (the *executor dativus*, appointed by the judge, as the *tutor dativus*); *lais ou clercs, ſeculiers ou reguliers* → "laymen or clerks, secular or regular"; *religieux de licence … de leur ſuperieur* → "religious, with leave … of their superior"; *cordeliers* → "Cordeliers" (the French name of the Franciscans, from their knotted cord); *gardien de ſainct François* → "Guardian of Saint Francis" (the superior of a Franciscan house); *nul oppoſant, ou contrediſant* → "no one opposing or gainsaying"; *l'eſtat & perfection … de leur reigle* → "the estate and perfection … of their rule" (the Franciscan vow of poverty, which bars the handling of money); *preſtres & clers ſacrez* → "priests and clerks in holy orders"; *tabellion ou notaire* → "tabellion or notary"; *reçoyuent inſtrumens* → "receive instruments" (draw up deeds); *rendre compte & preſter le reliqua* → "render account and pay over the balance remaining" per the glossary (*prestation de reliqua*); *tuteurs* → "guardians"; *inuentaire* → "inventory"; *baillee par le droit* → "given by the law"; *Eueſque* → "Bishop", Coras's capital kept; *pitoyables volontez* → "pious wills" (*pitoyable* in its old sense of pious, charitable; cf. *œuures pies* → "pious works"); *admonneſter* → "admonish"; *lais* → "bequests"; *emolument* → "profit" per the glossary; *ſ'en meſler* → "to meddle in it"; *defenſes* → "prohibitions"; *ineptitude* → "ineptitude"; *les ſainctes conſtitutions* → "the holy constitutions" (Justinian's Novels, cited at *r*, *ſ*, *x*); *Metropolitain* → "Metropolitan"; *le Iuge conſul, ou autre magiſtrat* → "the Judge, consul or other magistrate" (the municipal consuls of the Midi, who had a jurisdiction); *appellez les heritiers* → "the heirs … having been called" (summoned to the appointment). The one `[unclear]`: *telles defenſes ſont priuées* — "private" is the literal sense of *priuées* but sits ill between *peu raiſonnables* and *ineptitude*; a form of *prave* (perverse) or a dropped word is possible; translated literally and marked.
