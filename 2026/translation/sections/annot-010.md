⟦p025⟧Great certainly was the cunning of this lecher, to keep the said de Rols in this error for three years and more, so that she infallibly believed that she was his wife. But because where there is error we say that there is no consent, nor will {a}, and that evil deeds are not committed without deliberate purpose and intention to do wrong {b} — an adultery in particular, or other kind of lechery {c} — this woman here, as we shall discourse at length in its proper place, deserved by reason of this error some excuse. Which Pope Alexander III seems to have formally decided and determined {d}; for both the Popes and the Emperors too, in like terms, excuse the woman who remarries, thinking, with several others who also believe it, that her spouse is dead {e}, especially if the husband had remained four years or more abroad, and at the war {f}. Excused also is the virgin who marries a man already married, if she thought he was free to marry {g}. And the husband who finds his sister-in-law in his bed, and has to do with her, believing that it is his wife, is likewise excused {h}. And Lot was not punished for having had to do with his two daughters, who by stealth had put themselves in his bed, because he thought he was lying with his wife {i}; nor Jacob either, going in to Leah, because he believed he had Rachel beside him. {k}
