⟦p088⟧Who, arrived from Spain, having a wooden leg, as a year before had been set down by the soldier (of whom mention has been made above), presents a petition narrating the whole imposture, asking to be heard. The Court orders that he shall be heard, holding him under close arrest [*l'arrest clos*: confinement to an assigned place] in the keeping of the guard of the palace.

## Notes
The print heads this section TEXTE, (with a comma, as printed) at the top of p088, after Annotation LXVII (the print's LXVIII); the section carries no marginal citations. It completes the sentence left open at the end of texte-65 (*fait … apparoiſtre le vray Martin Guerre, Lequel…*). The `⟨alt⟩` at *narratine⟨alt:narratiue⟩* is taken as *narratiue* ("narrating"): *narratine* is no word, a turned *u*. The print opens a parenthesis before *duquel* and closes it after *parlé*, with a comma before it; kept. *Arriué des Eſpagnes* → "arrived from Spain" (Coras's plural *les Espagnes* rendered "Spain", per the case file §4); *vne iambe de bois* → "a wooden leg"; *conſigné par le ſoldat* → "set down by the soldier" (*consigner* → to set down, per the glossary at texte-21: the soldier from Rochefort of texte-32, whose report that Martin Guerre was in Flanders with a wooden leg was never heard at first hand; the case file §3); *preſente requeſte narratiue de toute l'impoſture* → "presents a petition narrating the whole imposture" (*requeste* → petition per the glossary); *requerant eſtre ouy* → "asking to be heard" (*estre ouy* → to be heard, per §5); *qu'il ſe feroit ouyr* → "that he shall be heard"; *luy tenant l'arreſt clos chez la garde du palais* → "holding him under close arrest in the keeping of the guard of the palace", with the French glossed in brackets on this first use. Here *arrest* is not the Court's *arrest* → "decision" of the house rule (§5) but the second sense Coras himself distinguishes in the next annotation — a magistrate's injunction to a person not to stir from an assigned place; the English keeps the word "arrest" so that Annotation LXVIII, which turns on the word, can be read. *La garde du palais* is the guard of the palace of the Parlement, where the prisoner was held in the Conciergerie (§4, §5). Ringold and Lewis: "while holding him in legal custody under the palace guard".
