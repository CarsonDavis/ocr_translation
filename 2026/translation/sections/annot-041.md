⟦p062⟧In this case, it seems that a proof by rumour and common repute ought not to be of small force: for we are in ⟦p063⟧a matter most ambiguous, monstrous and perplexed; in which acts, inasmuch as the certainty of things cannot be recovered except with great difficulty, rumour and common repute make sufficient proof {a}: as to show that Antoine is the son of Pierre, or that François is the son of Jean, or any other filiation {b}; or indeed to prove the death of someone.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. XLI. on p062, correctly. The marker alphabet restarts at *a*; the body carries the markers {a} and {b} only, on p063, and both are served by keyed notes. A third note on p063, "Bartole en la. ij. P. ſi dubitetur…", is printed with the key letter *o* where the run wants *c*, and has no marker anywhere in the body (the paragraph ends "de quelq'vn." with a plain full stop): a wrong sort for the key, and a marker the compositor dropped. It is listed below as an orphan; by its content it evidently serves the last clause, "to prove the death of someone". The page break falls between *en* and *vn faict* and is kept there; the transcription notes that the *n* of that *vn* may be printed without its arch, which does not affect the sense. Reflow gaps read through: *Antoi ne*, *au tre*. The print sets a point after {a} ("preuue {a}. cõme") and after {b} ("filiatiõ {b}. ou biẽ"), rendered by a colon and a semicolon, since the sentence continues. *Bruit & fame* → "rumour and common repute" per the glossary; *de petite vertu* → "of small force"; *faict fort ambigu, monſtrueux & perplex* → "a matter most ambiguous, monstrous and perplexed" (*perplex* of the case, cf. *perplexité* in the glossary); *eſquels actes* → "in which acts"; *la certitude des choſes ne ſe peut recouurer* → "the certainty of things cannot be recovered"; *fait ſuffiſante preuue* → "make sufficient proof" (cf. *faire preuve* at annot-027); *filiatiõ* → "filiation"; *ou biẽ* → "or indeed". The example names *Antoine, Pierre, François, Iean* are the jurists' stock names and are kept in French.

- {a} (p063): **The Doctors (commentators) on Decretals X 2.20 (*De testibus et attestationibus*), c. *Veniens* — the first of the chapters so beginning in that title (chapter number unverified) — and X 2.20.27, c. *Praeterea* (Celestine III: carnal union proved by hearsay testimony where *fama* and other aids concur)** — Les Docteurs au c. veniens. i. & au c. præterea. de teſtibus. [The canonists' doctrine that in matters hard to prove directly, common repute (*fama*) suffices; c. *Praeterea, de testibus* is one of the marginalia decoded in the case file §6.3.]
- {b} (p063): **Decretals X 2.19.10 (*De probationibus*, c. *Per tuas*: one commonly reputed a man's son, and acknowledged by him, is not disowned by his later oath)** — c. per tuas. de proba. [Filiation proved by common repute.]
