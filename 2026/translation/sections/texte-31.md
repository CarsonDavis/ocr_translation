⟦p053⟧Fourthly, the shoemaker who shod Martin Guerre deposes that this Martin was shod at twelve points, whereas nevertheless the prisoner is shod only at nine. And another, that the said Martin played well at fencing and at *palestrine* [wrestling], at which game the prisoner neither does nor understands anything. For the fifth, there are three witnesses, to one of whom, called Jean Espagnol, innkeeper of Touges, the said du Tilh disclosed himself on his return, begging him to say nothing of it: for Martin Guerre was dead, who had given him his property. To the o⟦p054⟧ther, called Valentin Rougié, who named him and recognised him as du Tilh, he made a sign with his finger that he should hold his peace. To the third, called Pelegrin de Liberos, he made a like sign and moreover gave two handkerchiefs, with the charge to deliver one to Jean du Tilh his brother.

## Notes
The print heads this section TEXTE. on p053. The page break falls inside *l'au|tre* and is kept inside "o|ther". Our print reads *Touges* here, not the *Tonges* the case file (§3, §4) records from the 1618 edition; the modern form is kept. *Poincts* are the shoemaker's points (case file §8). *Paleſtrine* is glossed in brackets: the *palaestra*, wrestling (Ringold and Lewis leave it "palestrine"). The print's "luy fit pareil & ſigne en outre, donna" has the *&* displaced; it is read as *luy fit pareil signe, & en outre donna*. The transcription records a stray raised apostrophe sort after *mouchoirs*, without meaning.
