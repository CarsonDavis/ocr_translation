⟦p068⟧The ruse of this rogue is marvellous, and such that, if ever a malefactor could deserve some excuse for being excellent and sovereign in his kind of artifice and wickedness, this one would be worthy of it above all oth⟦p069⟧ers: being another Phrynondas, of whom Aristophanes speaks, or a true Sisyphus,

A Sisyphus on earth, than whom none other more cunning (*Sisyphus in terris, quo non astutior alter*).

Inasmuch as the substitution being already discovered, he nevertheless knew so well how to impose upon the Judges, nay, even upon the said de Rols, whose husband he always said he was, that the Judges in effect released him by an interlocutory order upon contrary facts; and the woman again received him as Martin Guerre her husband.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. XLVII. on p068. The body carries no marker; the margin of p068 carries one note, keyed *a*, with no marker anywhere in the body (the transcription marks it an orphan). It is listed below as an orphan; by its content it serves the clause "if ever a malefactor could deserve some excuse for being excellent and sovereign in his kind". The page break falls inside *au|tres* and is kept at the corresponding word ("oth⟦p069⟧ers"). Reflow gaps read through: *mal faicteur*, *ma ri*, *appoin tement*. The Latin hexameter is set by the print as an indented line between two paragraph breaks, kept; it is translated in the text with the Latin in parentheses, being one line. *Paillard* → "rogue" per the glossary (*paillard* → lecher, rogue; here the sense is the trickster); *ruſe* → "ruse"; *eſmerueillable* → "marvellous" per the glossary; *mal faicteur* → "malefactor"; *excellent & ſouuerain en ſon eſpece d'artifice & meſchanceté* → "excellent and sovereign in his kind of artifice and wickedness" (*meschanceté* → wickedness); *à ceſtuy-ci en ſeroit ſur tous autres digne* → "this one would be worthy of it above all others"; *Phrinondas* → Phrynondas, the proverbial villain of Attic comedy (Aristophanes, *Thesmophoriazusae* 861; also Plato, *Protagoras* 327d); *Syſiphe* → Sisyphus; *la ſuppoſition* → "the substitution" per the glossary; *impoſer aux Iuges* → "to impose upon the Judges" (as at annot-005, *imposer*); *de laquelle ſe diſoit touſiours mari* → "whose husband he always said he was"; *relaxerent* → "released"; *appointement de contraires* → "interlocutory order upon contrary facts", glossed at texte-45; *en effect* → "in effect"; *la femme encor le receut* → "the woman again received him". The "Judges" here are those of the Seneschal's court, not the Parlement.

- Verse (p069): **A Latin hexameter on Sisyphus — source not identified; it adapts Homer, *Iliad* 6.153, where Sisyphus is "the craftiest of men" (*ὃ κέρδιστος γένετ' ἀνδρῶν*)** — Syſiphus in terris, quo non aſtutior alter. [The line is not in the *Ilias Latina* in this form; it may be a humanist rendering or Coras's own.]
- {a} (p068) — orphan note: printed in the margin of p068 with the key *a*, but with no marker in the body; by its content it serves the clause on the malefactor excellent in his kind: **Digest 48.19 (*De poenis*), l. *Ad bestias* (D. 48.19.31, Modestinus)** — l. ad beſtias D. de pœn. [Those condemned to the beasts are not to be released to please the crowd; but if they are of such strength or skill that they may worthily be exhibited to the Roman people, the governor is to consult the emperor — the malefactor's excellence in his kind earning him a reprieve, which is Coras's ironic point.]
