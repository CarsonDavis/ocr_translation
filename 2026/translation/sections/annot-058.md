⟦p078⟧It is a maxim spread through all the parts of the law, That more faith must be given to two witnesses who assure and affirm something than to six hundred or to a thousand who deny {a}; inasmuch as, as the Philosopher says, he knows better, and with greater certainty, who understands the thing in question by affirmation and assurance, than he who knows it by denial {b}. For also, by the nature of things, it is almost impossible to prove a negation: for how could it be proved that Antoine had never been at Toulouse? Contrariwise, therefore, where the difficulty of proof would not arise, this rule too would have no place {d}. Example: in a college of a university, to examine a scholar and approve him for the degree of Doctorate, seven doctors at the least are necessary {e}; of whom, if three assure his sufficiency and approve him, and the four others deny it and reject him, the testimony of these latter, as being the greater in number, will indubitably prevail {f}. Moreover, one must understand the decision of Accursius {g} [to hold] when the witnesses depose to a vague and uncertain negation: for if they restricted it to certain places, times and persons, inasmuch as, being by that means narrowed, it can easily be proved {h}, the witnesses also who deposed to it would be no less credible than those who deposed to the affirmation {i}. As for example, if Antoine is accused of having murdered Pierre in Toulouse, this first day of October 1560, and some witnesses so depose, and on the contrary others say that Antoine did not do this murder, for they saw him that day at Paris: if they are equal in number, they are as credible (and the more so because they depose for innocence {k}) as those who would testify for the accusation and the charge {l}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOT. LVIII. on p078. The marker alphabet restarts at *a* and runs a–l (no *j*), all on p078. The body carries the markers a, b, d, e, f, g, h, i, k, l; the margin carries notes a–l including a note *c*, which has no marker in the body (the transcription marks it an orphan). By its content note *c* — the Code's rule that *negantis factum probatio nulla sit* — serves the clause "by the nature of things, it is almost impossible to prove a negation", between {b} and {d}; it is listed below as an orphan at its evident place. Every other marker is served.
