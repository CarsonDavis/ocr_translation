⟦p064⟧I do not remember having read that any man had a memory so happy as to remember so many particular acts, places and words, from so long a time, and in regard to so many persons, save the Emperor Hadrian {a}. For Cyrus, King of the Persians, being in his great and numerous army, could well tell all the names of his soldiers and men-at-arms, and, reviewing his army, spoke to each one by his name {b}. Which Lucius Scipio also once did well at Rome {c}. Mithridates well remembered twenty-two languages, of as many nations as he had under him, speaking to each one without an interpreter {d}. Cineas, ambassador of Pyrrhus, in one day that he was at Rome, learned well all the names of the Roman Senators and knights {e}. Seneca could well, as he himself boasts, recite two thousand names in the same order in which they had been pronounced to him, and two hundred verses backwards, beginning from the last {f}. Which before him Theodectes, disciple of Aristotle {g}, and Metrodorus the Philosopher (who flourished in the time of Diogenes the Cynic) also did well {h}. One likewise praises much the memory of Julius Caesar, Scipio, Lucullus, Hortensius and Porcius Latro, Romans; and of Themistocles, Carneades and Charmides, Greeks {i}. But the memory of this du Thil here, whether he had gained it by art or by practice, surpassed [theirs], as it seems, he having never been found by the commissioners to have failed by a single iota. Which I mean to have written with the protestation I have made above {k}, of not wishing to enter into comparison of so impudent a cheat with persons so noble, great and illustrious.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. XLII. on p064. The marker alphabet restarts at *a* and runs a–k (no *j*), all on p064; every note is keyed and every marker served. Wrong sorts and slips translated by sense: *faiſanr* for *faiſant*; *Ariſtore* for *Ariſtote*; *aut* for *art* ("par art, ou par vſage"); *Luculle.,* with a stray point; in the margin *an* for *au* (note *h*), *arno tation* for *annotation* (note *k*), and *lieu vj* where the citation wants *liu. vij* (note *i*). Note *h* is cut short in the transcription at "al-" (*allegué*). Reflow gaps read through: *Ale xandrin*, *Tuſcula nes*, *arno tation*. The print sets a point after {a} ("l'Empereur {a}. car"), after {g} ("d'Ariſtore {g}. & Metrodore") and after {i} ("Grecs {i}. mais"), rendered by a full stop, a comma and a full stop as the sentences require. *La memoire ſi heureuſe* → "a memory so happy" (cf. *l'heur & la felicité de sa memoire* at annot-021); *exercite* → "army"; *genſdarmes* → "men-at-arms"; *faiſant la reueuë* → "reviewing"; *cheualiers Romains* → "Roman knights" (the *equites*); *au rebours* → "backwards"; *gaigné par art, ou par vſage* → "gained by art or by practice" (the art of memory, or exercise); "[theirs]" supplies the object of *ſurpaſſoit*; *n'ayant eſté iamais deſcouuert … qu'il euſt failli* → "having never been found … to have failed"; *d'vn ſeul iota* → "by a single iota"; *affrõteur* → "cheat" per the glossary; *du Thil* is the print's variant of the name, kept as at annot-035. The names are given in their standard English forms: *Adrian* → Hadrian, *Luce Scipion* → Lucius Scipio, *Cyneas* → Cineas (as at annot-021), *Theodectes*, *Metrodore* → Metrodorus, *Iule Ceſar* → Julius Caesar, *Luculle* → Lucullus, *Hortenſe* → Hortensius, *Carneades*; *Charmides* is kept as printed, though the Greek in Cicero's lists is Charmadas (see note *i*). The roll of memories repeats, with additions, the one at annot-021, to which note *k* refers back.
