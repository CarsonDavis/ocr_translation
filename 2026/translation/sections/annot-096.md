⟦p135⟧A pretext of marriage it truly was, for of lawful conjunction, Martin Guerre being alive, there could be none {a}: for several reasons, of which there are two principal ones. The first, that a woman cannot have two husbands {b}; nay, it is not credible that she should desire them {c}, unless it were in the country of the Medes, where the women are brought up in such an opinion that nothing more fortunate or honourable can befall them than for each to have several husbands: indeed, to have fewer than five seems to them a thing as it were ignominious, calamitous and wretched {d}. The second reason, that although there might have been a marriage, there was error in the person, which error hinders the will, and in consequence the marriage {e}, which takes its essence from the will and consent of the parties {f}. It will perhaps be objected to me that between Jacob and Leah there was a marriage, although Jacob thought to espouse and lie with Rachel, for whom he had served Laban his father-in-law seven years — who nevertheless, on the wedding night, substituted for Jacob his elder daughter Leah in place of Rachel the younger {g}. But the answer is plain: that although at the beginning Jacob had not consented to Leah, nevertheless afterwards he received her as his wife, approving the substitution and the marriage, which suffices {h}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOT. XCVI. on p135; the transcription numbers it 95 (the printed numbers run one ahead of the count from the eighty-fourth annotation on, as the earlier reports record). It expounds the words *Sous pretexte de mariage* of the decision (texte-94). Markers a–h on p135, all keyed and served. Reflow gaps read through: *reſpon ſe* (*reſponſe*), *fem me* (*femme*). *Pretexte de mariage, eſtoit il veritablement* → "A pretext of marriage it truly was"; *legitime conionction* → "lawful conjunction"; *ou fut au pays des Medes* → "unless it were in the country of the Medes" (*fut* = *fût*); *nourries à telle opinion* → "brought up in such an opinion" (*nourrir* in the sense of rearing, as at texte-36); *erreur en la perſonne* → "error in the person" (the *error personae*; *erreur* → "error" per the glossary, texte-10); *empeſche* → "hinders"; *ſuppoſa … ſa fille aiſnée Lea* → "substituted … his elder daughter Leah" (*supposer* → to substitute, per the glossary); *la ſuppoſition* → "the substitution"; *iaçoit* → "although". The Jacob and Leah example was used at annot-010 and annot-081, whose wording for the names and for *supposer* is followed. Coras's "which suffices" (*ce que ſuffit*) — subsequent consent cures the initial error — is the point on which the next annotations turn against Bertrande: she too "approved" the error by not contradicting it (annot-097).
