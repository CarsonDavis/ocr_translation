⟦p050⟧A witness is not credible, nor worthy of faith, who does not render a reason for his saying, {a} of which the Judge or the commissioner ought to interrogate him {b}; otherwise, if the commissioner has been so gross as not to ask it, the witness is not bound to render it {c2}, because he ought not to show himself eager [*affectionné*] to answer upon that on which he is not required or sought {d}. Nevertheless the witness also does no ill if he renders a reason voluntarily and of himself, without being interrogated upon it. Nay, in criminal matters (of which we treat here) the witness is bound to give a reason, even though he be not required to; otherwise his saying makes no faith {f}.
