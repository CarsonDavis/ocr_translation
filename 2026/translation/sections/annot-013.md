⟦p028⟧The King's procurator in France is he who prosecutes crimes as regards public vengeance; and a private person with an interest can prosecute only civilly, for his own interest {a}, and thus cannot conclude for a capital penalty ⟦p029⟧of blood or of death, but only for amends, either pecuniary, in money, or honourable, in asking pardon.

## Notes
- {a} (p028): **Guillaume Benedicti (Guillaume Benoît, 1455–1516), *Repetitio* on the chapter *Raynutius*, Decretals X 3.26 (*De testamentis*), c. 16, on the words *mortuo itaque testatore*, no. 159** — Guillaume Benedict. au ch. Raynutius ſur ceſte parole, mortuo itaq; teſt. i. & nõbre 159. de teſta. [Benoît's vast commentary on the decretal *Raynutius*, first printed at Lyon in 1522, is the standard French repository on the point; the number 159 is unverified.]
