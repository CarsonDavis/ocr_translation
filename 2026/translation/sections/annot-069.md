⟦p088⟧This word *Arrest*, in our French tongue, is taken in two ways. The first, for a final judgment and decree of a sovereign court; and so many think that it is drawn from the Greek ἄρεστον, which is as much as to say an ordinance of a magistrate. Whence it seems also, says that great Budé, that we err in writing and pronouncing it with a double R, seeing that ἄρεστον, from which it is drawn, is written only with a single R {a}. The second, for a kind of imprisonment, proceeding from the command and injunction of the magistrate made to some person not to stir from a certain place which he assigns to him {b}: as in our context.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. LXIX. on p088; it is the sixty-eighth in the running count (the print's numbers run one ahead of the count from Annotation LX onwards), and the transcription flags the number as uncertain. The marker alphabet restarts at *a*; the body carries {a} and {b}, both on p088, each served by a keyed note. The print sets a point after {b} ("aſſigne {b}. comme"), rendered by a colon, since the sentence continues. Reflow gaps read through: *iuge ment*, *ti ré*. The `⟨alt⟩` in note *b*, *lib.⟨alt:li. ho⟩ exhib.*, is left open: both readings abbreviate the same Digest title, *De homine libero exhibendo*, and the translation is unaffected. The word *Arreſt* is kept in italics as the French word under discussion, since the annotation is about the word itself; elsewhere the house rule renders Coras's *arrest* (the Court's judgment) "decision" (§5), and his second sense, in texte-66, "arrest". *Ceſte diction* → "This word"; *ſe prent en deux ſortes* → "is taken in two ways"; *vn dernier iugement, & decret d'vne cour ſouueraine* → "a final judgment and decree of a sovereign court" (*dernier* in the sense of last and final: the *arrest* is the judgment from which there is no appeal, as the case file §1 explains); *qui vaut autant à dire, comme* → "which is as much as to say" (Coras's formula, as at annot-070 for the inscription to Simon); *ordonnãce de magiſtrat* → "an ordinance of a magistrate"; *ce grãd Budée* → "that great Budé" (Guillaume Budé, 1467–1540, per §6.5); *nous faillons en l'eſcriuant, & prononçant par double R* → "we err in writing and pronouncing it with a double R" — Budé's etymology from ἀρεστόν (the neuter of ἀρεστός, "approved, resolved upon", whence "a thing decreed") would make the word *arest*; the received etymology is from the Latin *arrestare*, *ad-restare*, to stop or stay, which is the sense of Coras's second meaning, and the Greek derivation is not accepted today; the Greek is given as the print accents it (ἄρεστον, with the accent on the first syllable); *vne eſpece de priſon* → "a kind of imprisonment" (*prison* in the sense of detention, not the building); *commandement & inionction* → "command and injunction"; *de ne bouger d'vn certain lieu, qu'il luy aſſigne* → "not to stir from a certain place which he assigns to him"; *comme en noſtre contexte* → "as in our context" — the *arrest clos* of texte-66.
