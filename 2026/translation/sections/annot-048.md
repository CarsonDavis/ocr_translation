⟦p069⟧He was therefore a false attorney; for so is called, in our law, not only he who has never had, either before or after, charge from the person whose attorney he says he is {a}, or indeed who once had it but has had it revoked, as he well knew {b}; but also he who has overstepped the ends and bounds of his power, or who had no charge at the time of the execution {c}; nay, even he who had been well and legitimately appointed, if he did not know it and nevertheless did the acts of an attorney {d}. True it is that in this case Pierre Guerre, a false attorney at the beginning, has ⟦p070⟧been ratified by the said de Rols, and so all that had been done by him was confirmed and approved {e}; considering singularly that the acts of attorney done by the said Guerre, before the charge or procuration of the said de Rols, were done by authority of the Judge of Rieux — which comes into some consideration: for if they had been done by authority of the Court, that might have admitted of some doubt, inasmuch as the law holds in such great horror and detestation obreption, and a fault committed in the consistory of the prince {f}, that what is done by a false attorney in the audience-chamber of the prince cannot (as many think) be confirmed by any subsequent ratification {g},

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. XVIII. on p069 — a wrong number: it is the forty-eighth, between XLVII and XLIX, and the transcription marks the printed number uncertain; the heading should be read ANNOTATION XLVIII. The marker alphabet restarts at *a* and runs a–g across p069–p070; every note is keyed and every marker served. The annotation ends, in the print, with a comma after {g}: the sentence is left unfinished before the next TEXTE., and the comma is kept. The page break falls between *a* and *eſté* (*a eſté ratifié*) and is kept at the corresponding words ("has ⟦p070⟧been ratified"). The `⟨alt?:⟩` in note *d* (*Ꝑ.⟨alt?:P.⟩*) is unconfirmed by the transcribers on either side; it is the paragraph sign, which this print sets as a swash italic *P* and which the house rule (conventions §2) transcribes *P*; reading B is taken, and the translation is the same either way (§ *Sed et si quidem*). Wrong sorts and slips translated by sense: *procureut* and *procueur* for *procureur*; *Ceſtoit* for *C'eſtoit*; *d'aultresfois* for *d'autresfois*; in the margin *i lec* and *il lac* for *illec* ("there", i.e. Accursius and the Gloss on that law), *fal ſus* for *falſus*, *ca. dau.* for *cau. dat.*, *Iud. ſol.* for *iud. ſolui*, and *a iij.* in note *g* read as *alij* (§ *Sunt et alii*). Reflow gaps read through: *ap pellé*, *en cor*, *ſin gulierement*, *procura tori*. *Faux procureur* → "false attorney" (the *falsus procurator* of the Roman texts; *procureur* of a private party → attorney per the glossary); *charge* → "charge" (the mandate); *reuoquee* → "revoked"; *outrepaſſé les fins & bornes de ſa puiſſance* → "overstepped the ends and bounds of his power"; *au temps de l'execution* → "at the time of the execution" (of the act done); *bien & legitimement conſtitué* → "well and legitimately appointed"; *ratifié / ratification* → "ratified / ratification" (the *ratihabitio* of note *e*); *cõfirmé, & approuué* → "confirmed and approved"; *attendu ſingulierement* → "considering singularly"; *la charge ou procuration* → "the charge or procuration"; *vient en quelque conſideration* → "comes into some consideration"; *euſt peu receuoir quelque doute* → "might have admitted of some doubt"; *obreption* → "obreption" (the obtaining of a grant by a false statement, the term of the law of rescripts cited at {g}); *cõſiſtoire du prince* → "consistory of the prince" (the emperor's council sitting as a court, as in the Code title cited at {f}); *auditoire du prince* → "audience-chamber of the prince"; *ainſi que pluſieurs penſent* → "as many think"; *ratification ſuyuante* → "subsequent ratification". The "Court" whose authority would have made the matter doubtful is the Parlement; the point is that an act done before an inferior judge on a false procuration is curable by ratification, whereas (many hold) one done in the prince's own court is not.
