⟦p055⟧This proof also was not very conclusive: for it often happens that true and legitimate children take after a stranger better than their natural father, as we have shown above. Wherefore I shall content myself for the present with referring the reader to what Plutarch has written of it.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. XXXIIII. on p055, correctly. The print's marker alphabet is defective here: the margin carries a note keyed *a* (Plutarch, on the placita of the philosophers), but no marker *a* is printed anywhere in the body, which the transcription checked at high magnification; the note stands beside "referring the reader to what", where the sense wants it. The body is kept exactly as the French has it, without a marker, and the orphan note is listed below. "As we have shown above" refers to Annotation V (p019–p020), where Coras discusses why children resemble one parent, the other, or neither. *Rapporter* (of a child) → "to take after".
