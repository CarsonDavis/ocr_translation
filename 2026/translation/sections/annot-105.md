⟦p148⟧He could not have addressed himself better, inasmuch as friends, kinsfolk, household servants and neighbours are those who commonly know and understand the acts of the persons to whom they belong by right of kinship, friendship, familiarity or neighbourhood. Kinsfolk (say our Jurisconsults) have knowledge, in all likelihood, of what their kinsfolk do, of their estate, condition and quality {a}; and so likewise do neighbours among themselves {b}: so that he who says he does not know the estate or the deeds of his kinsman or neighbour cannot be excused under the pretext of the ignorance that he alleges {c}. Which must be understood to be true of things which in all likelihood fall within the knowledge of a kinsman or a neighbour: such as the health or the age of a kinsman {d}, the poverty or wealth {e}, dwelling {f}, marriage {g}, reputation and renown {h} of a neighbour; and generally all acts which cannot be done, dispatched or properly carried through save by the succession of time, requiring some length and interval {i}. For the things which are done among kinsfolk or in the neighbourhood, and can be brought very soon to an end and completed in a little time, do not easily pass into the knowledge of the other kinsfolk and neighbours, because they are most often done secretly — as a contract, a will, a crime {k}. For even what is done in public once only is not to be presumed to be known by all persons, seeing that such an act has had no duration {l}; although it is commonly said that of what is done in public no one may allege or pretend ignorance {m}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. CV. on p148; the transcription numbers it 104 (the printed numbering runs one ahead, flagged uncertain). The body carries twelve markers, {a}–{m} without *j*, all served by keyed notes. Two `⟨alt⟩` choices (one in the body, one in the margin of {g}), recorded in the batch report. Reflow gap read through: *per ſonnes*. One reading choice to note: *Les parens… ont cognoiſſance vraye, ſemblablement de ce que leurs parens font* is rendered "have knowledge, in all likelihood, of what their kinsfolk do", taking *vraye, ſemblablement* as the adverb *vray-ſemblablement* broken by a comma and a stray *e* — the jurists' rule that Coras is quoting is a presumption (kinsfolk are *presumed* to know), and he writes the same adverb with a space, *vray ſemblablement*, six lines on (*qui tombent vray ſemblablement en la cognoiſſance*); the alternative, "have true knowledge, likewise, of what…", would make the second clause (*& de meſme les voiſins*) redundant. *Il ne ſe pouuoit mieux adreſſer* → "He could not have addressed himself better"; *domeſtiques* → "household servants"; *par droit de parenté, amitié, familiarité ou voiſinage* → "by right of kinship, friendship, familiarity or neighbourhood"; *nos Iuriſconſultes* → "our Jurisconsults" per the glossary; *eſtat, condition, & qualité* → "estate, condition and quality"; *ſous pretexte de l'ignorance* → "under the pretext of the ignorance"; *reputation, & renommée* → "reputation and renown" (cf. *bruit & renommée*, annot-002); *qui ne ſe peuuent faire deſpeſcher, ou expliquer proprement: mais par la ſucceſſion de temps* → "which cannot be done, dispatched or properly carried through save by the succession of time" (*expliquer* in the Latin sense of *explicare*, to carry out; *mais par* = "but [only] by"); *deſirans quelque longueur, & interualle* → "requiring some length and interval"; *menées bien toſt à fin, & paracheuees* → "brought very soon to an end and completed"; *n'eſt à preſumer eſtre ſceu de toutes perſonnes* → "is not to be presumed to be known by all persons"; *n'a point eu de durée* → "has had no duration"; *alleguer, ou pretendre ignorance* → "allege or pretend ignorance".
