⟦p102⟧Women have this peculiar to them, says the Philosopher, that they believe lightly, and are easy to be deceived by the ruses and wiles of men {a}.

Such, to be sure, was the nature of the men of old: to deceive the female kind, those credulous hearts {b}.

And the Emperor Justinian said: we have sufficiently known the feeble nature of women, subject to a thousand trickeries and circumventions {c}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOT. LXXVI. on p102; the transcription numbers it 75 (the printed numbering runs one ahead, flagged uncertain). The body carries the markers {a}–{c}, all on p102, each served by a keyed note. The Latin elegiac couplet is set by the print as a paragraph of its own and is kept so; being two lines, it is translated in the text and the Latin given in the Notes. One `⟨alt⟩` marker falls in the margin (note *c*); the choice is in the batch report. This is the sentence the case file §10 quotes against Coras: women "easily deceived by the cunning and craftiness of men". *Ont cela de peculier* → "have this peculiar to them"; *le Philoſophe* → "the Philosopher" (Aristotle, as the schools called him; Coras's capital kept); *croyent de leger* → "believe lightly" (cf. *deposer à credit*); *faciles à eſtre deceuës* → "easy to be deceived"; *ruſes & cautelles* → "ruses and wiles" per the glossary; *la foible nature des femmes* → "the feeble nature of women" (the civil law's *fragilitas sexus*); *tromperies & circonuentions* → "trickeries and circumventions" (*tromperie* → trickery, *circonvenir* → to circumvent, per the glossary).

- {a} (p102): **Aristotle, *History of Animals* IX, at the opening (IX.1, 608b: the female is "more easily deceived", *εὐαπατητότερον*); the print's *de natura auiũ*, "of the nature of birds", is a wrong sort or slip for *animalium*** — Ariſtote au cõmencement du ix. liu. de natura auiũ. [Book IX opens with the characters of the two sexes: the female softer, more jealous, more querulous, more shameless, more false of speech, more easily deceived and of better memory.]
- {b} (p102): **Publio Fausto Andrelini (*Faustus*, of Forlì, c. 1462–1518), *Livia* (the *Amores*), book IV** — Fauſtus au iiij. de Liuie. [The couplet: *Scilicet ista fuit veterum natura virorum, / fallere femineum, credula corda, genus*. Andrelini's *Livia*, an elegiac collection in four books (Paris, 1490), was a school text in France in Coras's youth; the line and its place in book IV are unverified.]
- {c} (p102): **Novels (the *Authenticum*), collation 7, the title *De aequalitate dotis et propter nuptias donationis* (Nov. 97), at the paragraph *Quaesitum* (chapter unverified)** — P. quæſitũ. de æqua dor. aux nouuelles ſous la collation vij. [*De æqua dor.* = *De aequalitate dotis*; the *P.* is the print's sort for §. Justinian's legislation on the dowry repeatedly pleads the weakness of women and their exposure to deception as its ground.]
