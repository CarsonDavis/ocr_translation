⟦p073⟧Inasmuch as a witness ought to depose of what he can perceive and comprehend by one of his bodily senses, and not of what he has heard from another {a}. The testimony of having heard another say something, although it makes some sort of presumption {b}, nevertheless is not sufficient to make proof {c}; unless it were to show kinship or affinity, to the end of preventing some marriage {d} — and then, provided that such testimony be accompanied by rumour and common repute, together with other adminicles [supporting proofs] and circumstances {e2}; or else to make appear some very old and ancient fact {f}, as to prove the very ancient limits and bounds of one's lands {g}. And what is said above must be soundly understood of the testimony of having heard a third party say it; for as to having heard one of the parties pleading say it, the testimony would be very good {h}: as to have heard that Antoine promised a hundred *écus* to Pierre, which Pierre now demands of him; or else to have heard that Lucrece has espoused Camille by words of the present, and now this marriage between Camille and Lucrece is in question {i}.

## Notes

The marker alphabet restarts at *a* and runs, as the body is written here, a, b, c, d, e2, f, g, h, i; the margin has notes keyed *a*, *b*, *d*, *e2*, *f*, *g*, *h* and *i*. Antoine, Pierre, Lucrece and Camille are the stock names of a law-school example, not persons of the case.
