⟦p128⟧High justice is what the Jurisconsults call MERE IMPERE [*merum imperium*, pure power], that is to say a sovereign power of the sword, and of punishing anyone corporally by whipping of the body, cutting off of limbs, or, if ⟦p129⟧need be, by natural death {a}: of which justice, because the hangmen are the executors, they are called in France Executors of high justice.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. XCI. on p128; the body carries one marker, {a}, on p129, served by the one keyed note. Reflow gap read through: *appel lent* (*appellent*). *MERE IMPERE* is Coras's gallicised form of the Roman *merum imperium*, printed in capitals; it is kept as he prints it, with the Latin in brackets, since Coras goes on to gloss it himself. *Vne ſouueraine puiſſance du glaiue* → "a sovereign power of the sword" (Ulpian's *gladii potestas*); *fuſtigation de corps* → "whipping of the body"; *diſſection de membres* → "cutting off of limbs"; *mort naturelle* → "natural death" per the glossary (annot-012: *mort civile / mort naturelle*); *les Bourreaux ſont executeurs* → "the hangmen are the executors"; *Executeurs de la haute iuſtice* → "Executors of high justice", the literal rendering the annotation requires (see texte-89 and the glossary, where the phrase is otherwise rendered "executioner").

- {a} (p129): **Digest 2.1.3 (*De iurisdictione*, l. *Imperium*, Ulpian)** — l. iij. D. de iuriſdi. [*Imperium aut merum aut mixtum est. Merum est imperium habere gladii potestatem ad animadvertendum facinorosos homines*: power is either pure or mixed; pure power is to have the power of the sword to punish wicked men — the text Coras translates.]
