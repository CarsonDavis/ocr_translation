⟦p125⟧The crime of Enslavement also results from this deed; under which are bound ⟦p126⟧not only those who give, sell or buy a free person {a}, but also those who conceal, imprison or detain one, or otherwise abuse her {b}: a crime beyond doubt capital, deserving death by the law of God. WHOEVER SHALL HAVE stolen (says the Lord) any of his brethren, and afterwards sold him, or otherwise abused him, he shall die the death {c}; which singularly has place when such a theft has been made of a person near to, and closely joined with, another by some great bond of nature, as a child or a wife {d}. And it is nothing to say that the said de Rols was detained of her own will, seeing that she never gainsaid it {e}, but rather always vigorously maintained that it was her husband. For, by what has been said a little before, that is not, properly speaking, will, since it is forced and extorted by ruses, cunning and seductions {f}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. LXXXVI. on p125; by the true count it is the 85th (the print's numbers run one ahead from Annotation 84), and it expounds the word *Plaige* of the decision (texte-84). Markers a–f on p126, all keyed and served. The page break falls between *duquel* and *ſont obligez* and is kept at the corresponding words ("under which are bound ⟦p126⟧not only"). Reflow gaps read through in the margin: *Deuterono me*, *ti tre*, *volũ tatem*. Two `⟨alt⟩` readings in the body (*freres;* / *freres.*; *coniointe* / *conioincte*) and one in the margin (note *f*), none affecting the English; see the report. *Plaige* → "Enslavement" per the glossary, glossed at texte-84; *reſulte de ce faict* → "results from this deed"; *duquel ſont obligez* → "under which are bound" (*obligati*, liable to the crime's penalty); *recelent* → "conceal"; *en abuſent* → "abuse her" (the free person; the French pronoun is feminine for *perſonne*). The scripture is Deuteronomy 24:7, translated from Coras's French, whose capitals (*QVI AVRA*) are kept; *il moura de mort* → "he shall die the death". *ſingulierement a lieu* → "singularly has place"; *fort coniointe* → "closely joined"; *il n'y fait rien de dire* → "it is nothing to say"; *retenue de ſa volonté* → "detained of her own will"; *contredit* → "gainsaid" (the Digest's *nisi evidenter contradicat*, cited at *e*); *viuement … deffendu* → "vigorously maintained"; *ruſes, fineſſes, & ſeductions* → "ruses, cunning and seductions" per the glossary. "What has been said a little before" is Annotation 83, on consent extorted by fraud.

- {f} (p126): **Digest 43.29 (*De homine libero exhibendo*), l. 3, § *Si quis volentem* (D. 43.29.3.5, Ulpian), as at Annotation 83 {g}** — l. iij. P. ſi ꝗs volentem. D. de lib. ho. exhib. [The *ꝗ* is the *q* with a stroke, the sign for *qui-*: *si quis*.]
