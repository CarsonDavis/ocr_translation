⟦p097⟧These persons certainly deserved some excuse for not being willing to depose against their own brother; to which also the law has not willed to constrain them {a}, even when it is a matter of a thing so weighty as a capital crime {b}. And in truth it would be a thing too near to inhumanity to force a man to ruin and destroy his bones, his blood and his own flesh {c} — which no one, says the Apostle, ever held in hatred {d}, he esteeming him who has no care for his own to be worse than an infidel {e}. Which has moved our Interpreters to teach that he who has promised to lend the Prince some castle or fortress, generally against all comers, is nevertheless not bound to lend it against himself {f}, nor against his father, mother, children, brothers and other near kinsfolk, who by nature are scarcely less dear to him than himself {g}.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. LXXII. on p097; it is the seventy-first in the running count (the print's numbers run one ahead of the count from Annotation LX onwards), and the transcription flags the number as uncertain. The marker alphabet restarts at *a* and runs a–g, all on p097; every note is keyed and every marker served. Reflow is clean; the print's *ſ'agiſt* for *ſ'agit*, and in the margin *c ſi teſtes* without a point, *de iureiu* cut short (*de iureiurando*), *Barto in* for *Bartole en*, *accutiſsimi* for *acutissimi*, and an italic capital *P* for the paragraph sign § in note *a*. *Meritoyent certainement quelque excuſe* → "certainly deserved some excuse"; *depoſer contre* → "to depose against" (*deposer* → to depose per the glossary); *la loy ne les a pas voulu cõtraindre* → "the law has not willed to constrain them"; *choſe ſi importante, que d'vn crime capital* → "a thing so weighty as a capital crime"; *trop approchante de l'inhumanité* → "too near to inhumanity" (the *humanitas* of the Code law cited at {c}); *ruiner & deſtruire* → "ruin and destroy" (*ruyner & perdre* → to ruin and destroy, texte-18); *ſes os, ſon ſang, & ſa propre chair* → "his bones, his blood and his own flesh"; *laquelle perſonne, dit l'Apoſtre, n'euſt oncques en haine* → "which no one, says the Apostle, ever held in hatred" (Ephesians 5:29, "no man ever yet hated his own flesh"); *celuy qui n'a ſoin des ſiẽs* → "him who has no care for his own"; *pire qu'infidelle* → "worse than an infidel" (1 Timothy 5:8); *nos Interpretes* → "our Interpreters" (the glossators and commentators, as at annot-039 and annot-044); *preſter au Prince quelque chaſteau, ou fortereſſe* → "to lend the Prince some castle or fortress" (the feudal duty of *rendability*, to deliver one's castle to the lord on demand); *generalement contre tous* → "generally against all comers"; *n'eſt pourtant tenu* → "is nevertheless not bound" (*pourtant* here in its modern sense, the sentence being concessive; cf. the glossary's warning at annot-030); *prochains parens* → "near kinsfolk"; *gueres moins chers* → "scarcely less dear".
