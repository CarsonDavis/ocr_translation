⟦p102⟧Joined to the incredible longing which the same de Rols had to recover her husband: things which persuaded her too easily that the prisoner was he, especially as he gave several private and particular tokens; but from the moment she began to perceive the fraud, she wished a hundred thousand times for death, which she would have executed upon herself but for the fear of God.

## Notes
The print heads this section TEXTE. on p102, after Annotation LXXV (printed LXXVI); the section carries no marginal citations. It completes the sentence begun in texte-73 (*Ioinct…*, "Joined to…"), and its second half is the passage the case file §10 quotes as Coras's statement of Bertrande's motive, *l'incroyable envie, qu'icelle de Rolz avoit, de recouvrer son mary*. *Ioinct* → "Joined to"; *l'incroyable enuie… de recouurer ſon mary* → "the incredible longing… to recover her husband" (*envie* in the old sense of longing, as the case file renders it; cf. *envieuse de voir & recouvrer* → desirous of seeing and recovering, texte-06); *icelle de Rols* → "the same de Rols"; *perſuaderent trop facilement* → "persuaded her too easily"; *meſmes qu'il donnoit* → "especially as he gave"; *priuées & particulieres enſeignes* → "private and particular tokens" per the glossary (texte-07); *déſ lors qu'elle commẽça ſ'apperceuoir de la fraude* → "from the moment she began to perceive the fraud" (the same words return at annot-077); *ſouhaitta cent mille fois la mort* → "wished a hundred thousand times for death"; *euſt ſur ſoy-meſmes executée* → "would have executed upon herself"; *ſans la crainte de Dieu* → "but for the fear of God". The wish for death is what Annotation LXXVII (annot-076) then takes up at length.
