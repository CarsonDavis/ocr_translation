⟦p026⟧One might doubt here whether these children are legitimate, and dispute copiously on one side and the other; nevertheless, to be brief about it, and not to go looking for a knot in a bulrush, one must without difficulty believe that they are legitimate, by reason of the good faith of the wife, who thought she was having to do with Martin Guerre, her true husband {a} — just as also, if a woman married a priest whom she thought to be a lay person, and of a condition to marry, the children who should proceed from such nuptials [would be] legitimate {b}. For to render the children illegitimate and bastards, it would be necessary that both the father and the mother knew of the impediment and the fraud {c}. Added to which, in doubtful facts the law wills and ordains that the interpretation be taken in favour of the legitimacy of the children {h}, even though there were [ground to think] that they had been born of lechery {e}. It is true that if we are willing to give some credit to the Interpreters, this, which is certain and settled by the law of the Pontiffs, might admit of some controversy under the civil law: inasmuch as the Emperor has left it written that if a woman marries a slave, thinking to marry a free and freeborn person, and the truth is afterwards discovered, the dowry shall be returned to her, but the children who shall be born of this marriage shall be bastards and illegitimate {f}. Added to which, in another place, the Emperors Valentinian, Theodosius and Arcadius will that those who have contracted marriages forbidden by the law should prove clearly that they were placed in an error, not a simple one, but a very great and very just one {g}, showing thereby that an affected ignorance, and one very lightly occasioned, as this one might be, would not suffice. They add further: provided that, having understood the error, the married pair separate at once and without delay {h}. Which has not been done here. But as for me, it has always seemed to me that on this point there is no difference whatever between the civil and the canon laws. For Antoninus puts a special case, when the wife marries a slave, with whom everyone knows well that there is not, nor can be, any lawful marriage {k}. And as to the constitution of Valentinian {l}, it says nothing whatever about the children, whether they ought to be legiti⟦p027⟧mate or not, but only imposes a penalty on those who marry against the precepts and prohibition of the Law, as Accursius himself teaches. {m}
