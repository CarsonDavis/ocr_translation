⟦p128⟧The Court had at one time resolved that the public penance (*amende honorable*) should also be made at the bar of the audience chamber (*parquet de l'audience*), on the day of the pronouncing of the decision. But afterwards it was considered that this rash fellow was of a shameless impudence and an immeasurable overweening, presumptuous, virulent, and more abounding in petulance of language than a Theon or an Archilochus, and that he would at every word have disturbed Monsieur the President who was pronouncing, and the assembly: wherefore it was on the contrary resolved, for this sole reason, that they would content themselves with the penance which should be made at the place of Artigat, where he had offended.

## Notes
The print heads this annotation ANNOTAT. XC. on p128 (the print's numbering runs one ahead of the section id); it expounds the words *Du lieu d'Artigat* of the *arrest* (texte-88) and carries no marginal citations. Reflow gap read through: *conten teroit* (*contenteroit*). The print's full stop after *l'arreſt* ("de l'arreſt. mais apres") is kept as a sentence break. *Auoit vne fois arreſté* → "had at one time resolved" (*arrester* here in its ordinary sense of deciding, not the *arrest* of §5); *amende honorable* → "public penance (*amende honorable*)" per §5; *au parquet de l'audience* → "at the bar of the audience chamber", the enclosed floor of the courtroom where the *arrest* was pronounced, the French kept in parentheses on this first use; *la prononciation de l'arreſt* → "the pronouncing of the decision" per §5; *ce temeraire* → "this rash fellow" (*temerairement* → rashly, per the glossary); *impudence effrontée, & deſmeſurée outrecuidance* → "a shameless impudence and an immeasurable overweening" (cf. *impudence outrecuidée* → "overweening impudence", annot-007); *petulence de langage* → "petulance of language" (cf. *petulant & outrecuidé*, annot-008); *Theon* is the Roman grammarian whose biting tongue Horace made proverbial (*dente Theonino*, *Epistles* 1.18.82), and *Archilochus* the Greek poet of iambic invective — both bywords for slanderous abuse; *monſieur le preſident, qui prononçoit* → "Monsieur the president who was pronouncing" (*Monsieur* kept per §8; the first president, Jean de Mansencal, read the decision, §3); *l'aſſiſtance* → "the assembly" (those present at the pronouncing); *fut au cõtraire deliberé* → "it was on the contrary resolved"; *où il auoit delinqué* → "where he had offended" (*delinquere*, the Latinism of the civil law for committing the offence — the principle, developed at annot-091, that the penalty is executed where the crime was done).
