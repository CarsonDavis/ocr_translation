## For the reviewer

1. **Coras's cross-reference at annot-105 {a}, "annotation lxxi".** The annotation on magic is annot-070 in the transcription (p090–p096), whose printed head the transcription reads ANNOT. LXVI. — a wrong sort for LXXI, since the neighbouring heads are LXX (annot-069) and LXXII (annot-071). Coras's own reference therefore matches the *printed* numbering, not the transcription's count. The previous batch's report (texte-70–annot-077, item 2) inferred the opposite from annot-026 {b}; the two pieces of evidence conflict and the numbering question should be settled with both in view before the front matter is written. The reference at annot-106 {a} (XXXVII = annot-037, p058) does not discriminate: there the printed and counted numbers still agree.
2. **texte-102, the dropped verb.** *De ce qu'iceluy Martin | dire, & faire* — a word is missing at the foot of p147 / head of p148. Flagged `[unclear]` with "[was wont to]" supplied. The transcribers should check whether the print itself lacks it (a line dropped at the page turn, or a catchword mistaken for text) before the English is settled.
3. **annot-104, *vraye, ſemblablement*.** Read as the adverb *vray-ſemblablement* ("in all likelihood"); see the `[unclear]` section. If the house prefers the literal "true knowledge, likewise", the change is one phrase.
4. **annot-103, *nul ne ſçait*.** Kept literal ("no one knows the difference"); the reviewer may prefer "not everyone knows" with a note, which is plainly what Coras means.
5. **annot-103 {b}, the "Cicero" definition of comedy.** It is Donatus's, attributed to Cicero, and not in the *Pro Roscio*; the Notes say so. Worth a line in the apparatus, since the formula was a Renaissance commonplace.
6. **annot-105, Ovid twice.** The Latin line and Coras's French version of it are both translated (house rule: nothing omitted). If the duplication reads badly, the alternative is to drop the English in parentheses after the Latin line and let Coras's own rendering carry it.
7. **annot-107 {m}, *pro-Fotcio*.** Read as *Pro Fonteio*; the sentence attributed to Cicero was not located there or in the *Partitiones*. A classicist's check would settle whether Coras is quoting or paraphrasing — and from which speech.
8. **annot-107 {l}, *§ Mãconella*.** Unidentified; the word looks like a wrong sort. A canonist's check of X 5.1 under the chapters beginning *Cum* might recover it.
9. **annot-107 {a}, *l. addictos*.** The constitution *Addictos supplicio* is cited three times in the section under *C. de appellationibus*; its modern locus (C. 7.62 or C. 7.65) was not verified and is given without number.
10. **Names of the two friends.** The copy-text prints *Piuol* and *Guilher* on p145 and *Puiol* and *Quillet* on p147; the English follows each page ("Pivol", "Guilher"; "Pujol", "Quillet") and records the case file's forms (Pivol, Guilhet). The house may prefer one form throughout, with the variants in a note.
11. **texte-105, *neantmoins*.** Rendered "and moreover" (list continuation); the reviewer may prefer the literal "and nevertheless" with a note.
12. **annot-105, "Monsieur the President".** The president who put the question about the familiar spirit is not named; the case file §3 gives the first president as Jean de Mansencal, without warrant in the *Texte*. Left unidentified in the English, identified in the Notes as the case file's suggestion only.
