## For the reviewer

1. **texte-64, *en preuention de meſme faict*** → "under prosecution for the same deed". *Prevention* is the judge's cognizance of a criminal matter; the gloss is in brackets. But *de meſme faict* sits oddly beside texte-45's *pour autres faits* for the same Escornebœuf prosecution before the Seneschal. Either Coras means "the same matter" (the imposture, which the Seneschal's court had taken up) or *de meſme faict* is used loosely ("in the same way"). Translated literally; decide whether to annotate the tension.
2. **texte-66 / annot-068, *arrest*.** The house rule renders Coras's *arrest* "decision"; texte-66's *l'arreſt clos* is the other sense, which Annotation LXVIII expressly distinguishes, so the word "arrest" is kept there with a bracketed gloss, and the annotation keeps *Arrest* in italics as the word under discussion. Check that the front matter's note on *arrest* → "decision" mentions this second sense.
3. **annot-068, Budé's Greek etymology.** Coras (after Budé) derives *arrest* from ἄρεστον and says the double *R* is an error. The received etymology is Latin *arrestare*. The translation gives Coras's claim as he makes it and records the modern view in the headnote; decide whether that remark belongs in the apparatus or should be cut.
4. **annot-070, the misplaced paragraph break at p096.** The reflow starts a new paragraph at the top of p096 in the middle of *meſme-|ment*. Not kept (the word is kept whole). This is the one departure from "keep the French paragraphing"; if the house prefers strict adherence, the English would have to read "especial\n\n⟦p096⟧ly", which is not a paragraph. Better to fix the stitcher.
5. **annot-070, *amiable* for *admirable*** (p092). Read as a wrong sort and translated "astonishing", with the inference shown in brackets. Check the page image: if the print really has *amiable* the brackets stay; if *admirable*, the transcription should be corrected and the brackets removed.
6. **annot-070, the Faustina sentence** (p094) lacks its verb in the print; marked `[unclear]` with Capitolinus's sense. Check the page image for a dropped line.
7. **annot-070, Coras's misreferences.** "The thirteenth of the Aeneid" (Ecl. 8.69), "Virgil x" (Ecl. 8.70), "Ovid book II *de Ponto*" (*Ars am.* 2.105–106), "Augustine viij" (*City of God* XVIII), Pliny's two million lines for Zoroaster given as a hundred thousand, "C. xxxvj q. v" for C. 26 q. 5, Eusebius's *Ecclesiastical History* for Jerome's *Chronicle*. All left as he wrote them, with the correct loci in the Notes. This annotation shows Coras citing from memory or from a commonplace book (Crinito, Rhodiginus, Polydore Vergil, Maffei); the introduction may want to say so.
8. **annot-070, *reſueries* → "ravings" and *preſtiges* → "juggleries".** Both are period-flavoured choices; "delusions" and "illusions" would be flatter but plainer. The pair *preſtiges & illuſions* occurs three times and needs two distinct words.
9. **annot-070, Coras's view of the magic hypothesis.** He says there was "great reason" for the suspicion, that magic is real and diabolical, and never says whether du Tilh used it; the annotation ends in a prayer rather than a verdict. The case file §2 records du Tilh's denial of necromancy on the scaffold; §10 makes the prisoner's impossible knowledge Davis's first argument. The translation keeps the hesitation, and the apparatus should connect the three.
10. **annot-067, *Oculus Dei acutus eſt, videt omnia*.** Unsourced; probably a tag. If anyone recognises it (an emblem book, a schoolroom sentence collection), the Notes entry should be updated.
11. **annot-066, the orphan note *c*.** Placed after "which could cause it"; check the page image for the dropped marker.
12. **Numbering.** The print's annotation numbers run one ahead of the count from LX, and LXX is misprinted LXVI. The headnotes record both; the site should decide whether to display the printed number, the count, or both.
