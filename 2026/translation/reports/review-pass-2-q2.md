## Glossary decisions

1. **ſoubredens / ſoubre-dents → "supernumerary teeth"** (texte-40, texte-61). The same word was "extra teeth" at texte-40 and "supernumerary teeth" at texte-61; one English for the one word, and the record's register takes the technical term. §9 row amended; texte-40 edited.
2. **pourtant → context decides** between "for that reason" (the common 16th-c. causal sense, annot-030) and "nevertheless" (annot-054, *leſquels pourtant par ſuccez de temps … deuiennẽt gros*, which can only be adversative). The §9 row's "not 'however'" was too strong. Row amended; no section edit — annot-030 is left as settled, with a finding that "however" would read as naturally there.
3. **Palhe → Palhé.** Our print reads *Palhé* with the accent at p062; the case file's *Palhe* (§3, §4) came from the literature. Rows amended; the English already reads "Palhé [Pailhès]".

## The fixes (two in all), with French and both Englishes

**annot-033** (p054–055)
- FR: *En cor que ceſte preuue n'euſt pas eſté fort neceſſaire, par ce qu'vn teſmoignage d'auoir ouy dire ne preuue point*
- was: "Although this proof was not very necessary, because a testimony of having heard say [hearsay] proves nothing"
- now: "Although this proof was not very cogent [*neceſſaire*: compelling], because a testimony of having heard say [hearsay] proves nothing"
- *Preuve necessaire* is the schools' *probatio necessaria*, the proof that compels; "necessary" reads as "needed" and breaks the logic of the "because" clause.

**texte-40** (p061)
- FR: *Martin Guerre auoit deux ſoubredens à la machoire de deſſus*
- was: "Martin Guerre had two extra teeth in the upper jaw"
- now: "Martin Guerre had two supernumerary teeth in the upper jaw"
- Consistency with texte-61 (*ſoubre-dents* → "supernumerary teeth"). Ringold and Lewis have "two broken teeth in his lower jaw", wrong on both counts.

## Ringold–Lewis, where it matters

- **texte-44**: R&L make the thousand-deaths oath Bertrande's ("telling whoever was willing to believe her word that she would undergo a thousand cruel deaths rather than swear"). The French has du Tilh offering to abide by her oath and staking his life on it (*qui l'en voulut croire à ſon ſerment, ſe ſouſmettãt à mille morts cruelles, ſi elle iuroit*); she refuses to swear "nor believe him upon it either". Ours is right, and the point matters for §10 (her conduct at the confrontation).
- **texte-47**: "reversed judgment" for *appointement de contraires* (already in §2/§5); the direction of *ſouſminiſtrant … ledit Pierre Guerre* reversed; *on luy auança ce fait auſsi* dropped.
- **texte-48**: "co-guardian / guardian / guardianship" for *comprocureur / procureur / procure* (already in §5). See misreadings on *comprocureur*.
- **texte-53, 54**: "excused" for *reprochez*, "reasons" for *obiects*.
- **texte-40**: "broken teeth … lower jaw" for *ſoubredens … machoire de deſſus*.
- Lesser: texte-29 (result clause read as cause), texte-31 ("thrust forward" for *eſleué en haut*), texte-32 (*Tonges*), texte-42 (*dix* dropped), texte-43 (garbled sentence), texte-54 ("weight" for *groſſeur*).
